Djinkya's Last Play

1,493 words
Reading on

I know I deserve death but please listen I had exams and project exhibition so please forgive me. Join the discord if you wish to talk to me

(Flashback — Hospital wing, the night before discharge)

The gym cycle's wheel was still humming down to a stop when I dropped back onto the mattress. Four hours of pedaling like my life depended on it — because, it kind of did — and my legs had gone from aching to something past aching, a place where pain stops being a sensation and starts being more like hobby.

Outside the door, the corridor lights had dimmed to the shade hospitals use for "we would like you to be asleep now." A cart squeaked past without stopping. Nobody expected me to be awake either.

I stared at the ceiling until it stopped tilting.

"Open Archive," I said, once my lungs agreed to cooperate.

[ STAT WINDOW ]

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

KARMIC POINTS

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

**[Karmic Coins: 600]**

I looked at the number for a while. Six hundred was not nothing so functionally it might as well have been loose change I'd found in an old coat.

"Open the Wheel."

[You are aware it is two in the morning,] the Archive said. [You are aware you have surgery-adjacent injuries. You are aware that gambling has, historically, not gone well for you.]

"I am aware of all three and have chosen to proceed regardless."

[Noted, for the record, as 'host was warned.']

The Wheel opened in front of me, unbothered by the hour — four wedges of color, same as always.

Art. Skill. Artifact. ????.

It had landed on Skill, twice. Art, once. The sliver marked ???? exactly once, and that one had put me in a ten-hour coma and left something called Curse of Despair sitting in my chest like a tenant who'd stopped paying rent.

I did not examine why I kept coming back to a machine that had already hurt me once. That was a question for a version of me with more free time.

"Spin."

The Wheel turned.

It went longer than it needed to for four sections. Long enough that I had time to regret the decision, un-regret it, and start drafting an apology to my mother in my head, just in case.

Then — click.

[Segment: Artifact]

I sat up too fast. My ribs informed me.

"That's a first," I said, ignoring the ribs.

[Correspondingly,] the Archive said, and something in its tone had shifted — not warmer, exactly, but more careful, the way you get careful around something you haven't personally tested yet, [so is this.]

The air above the blanket folded, the way it always did before a reveal, except this time it kept folding — layer after layer, as if the notification itself hadn't decided how big it needed to be.

{Master Puppeteer's Set}

[Type: Artifact — Complete. Not fragmentary. Nothing here for you to break further.]

[Contents:]

2x Djinkya's Understudy (Puppet)

1x Djinkya's Cloth

1x Djinkya's Needle

I read it twice. "Who's Djinkya?"

[Give me a moment,] the Archive said. [Cross-referencing is slower when the source material predates my classification protocols by a considerable margin.]

A pause, longer than its usual pauses.

[Archive Log — Origin Fragment, partially recovered:]

(Djinkya, Master Puppeter and Actor…..[Additional information cannot be fetched])

Cause of death: unknown.

Last recorded act: one final play, written, cast, and never performed, because its author did not live to see opening night. He did not ask for more time. He asked for two understudies who would never miss a cue, a needle that would never let a seam come undone, and a curtain that could make a man vanish from the world entirely — because, in his own words, 'the best exit is the one no one sees you make.']

I sat with that for a second.

"That's — dramatic," I said, mostly to have something to say.

[He was a playwright,] the Archive said. [Drama was the job.]

The puppets came first — two of them, small enough to sit in my palms, dressed in matching threadbare coats, like they'd walked straight off a stage nobody had finished building. Glass eyes, painted just slightly too warm to be comfortable. They didn't move. I checked twice anyway.

{Djinkya's Understudy x2}

[Description: Crafted to play any part flawlessly. When bound to a target, mimics that target's form, mannerisms, and abilities — up to the limit of the puppet's own capacity.]

[Limitation: An understudy performs the role. It does not become the actor. Whatever the target can do, the puppet can only do as well as the puppet is capable of holding.]

"So they copy people," I said. "With a ceiling."

[Every actor has range,] the Archive said. [These two simply have less of it than the people they're impersonating. Cast accordingly.]

Next was the cloth — folded so small it looked like a handkerchief, except handkerchiefs don't usually make the hair on your arm stand up when you touch them.

{Djinkya's Cloth}

[Description: Worn, it removes the wearer's presence from the world entirely — sight, sound, memory, record — for a limited duration.]

[Limitation: The world does not enjoy being edited. Duration is short. Recovery after use is not instant, and the Archive cannot promise what 'presence' reconstructs to, precisely, once the world puts you back.]

"When it puts me back," I repeated slowly. "As opposed to."

[As opposed to nothing you need to think about tonight,] the Archive said, in the specific tone it used when it had decided a conversation was over.

Last was the needle. It didn't look dangerous, which by now I recognised as the entire design philosophy of everything in this box — no case, no warning label, just a long silver needle catching the one strip of moonlight the blinds let through.

{Djinkya's Needle}

[Description: Stitches anything to anything. The stitch does not come undone. Not with time. Not with force. Not with regret.]

[Warning: This item is cursed. The Archive does not use that word lightly, and it is not being used lightly now.]

I picked it up carefully, the way you pick up something that has already decided it doesn't need your permission to exist.

"Cursed how?"

[Unknown in scope. Known in principle,] the Archive said. [Permanence is not a small thing to hand a Thirteen-year-old with your track record for editing things until they break in interesting ways.]

"I'm fourteen."

[The math does not improve your case.]

Somewhere down the corridor a door opened and a pair of shoes started walking, unhurried, in the general direction of my room. I did the only reasonable thing available to a boy lying in a hospital bed at two in the morning holding two puppets, a cursed needle, and a piece of cloth that erased people: I shoved the whole set under the blanket and lay very still, doing my best impression of someone who had never been awake in his life.

The footsteps passed. A door closed somewhere else. I let out the breath I'd been holding.

[Smooth,] the Archive said.

"Shut up."

I sat back up, unloaded the set onto my lap, and looked at the final line still hanging patiently in the corner of the window.

[Claim Cost: 600 Karmic Coins]

[Deducted automatically. Complete artifacts do not negotiate.]

"Wait." I sat up straighter, which the ribs vetoed a second time. "Why Six hundred?"

[You spun the Wheel. That was you asking.]

“But usually only a hundred Karmic Coins get cut”

[Since always,] the Archive said. [You were simply too poor, previously, for it to come up.]

[Balance: 0 Karmic Coins]

I looked at the zero. It looked back, with the specific smugness only a balance of exactly nothing can manage.

"...Why Six hundred," I said slowly, "specifically for this time?"

There was a pause. The kind the Archive used right before it said something it knew it shouldn't.

[Inflation, my guy.]

"..."

[.........]

"You cannot just say that to me right now."

[I can. I did. You may grieve at your own pace.]

I lay back down, needle in one hand, two puppets and a fistful of cursed cloth balanced on my chest, a balance of zero coins glowing faintly in the corner of my vision like the world's smallest tombstone.

Tomorrow I'd be discharged. Tomorrow I'd have to walk into Professor Serin's office and explain — nothing, actually, since she had no idea any of this existed, which was, on reflection, a mercy I hadn't earned.

Tonight, I had two of a dead man's understudies, a needle that didn't believe in mistakes, and a cloth that could make me disappear from the world if I ever needed to badly enough.

Broke, exhausted, and technically richer than I'd been an hour ago in every way that couldn't be counted.

I'd take it.

I closed my eyes. Past the window, the sky over the medical wing was already starting to grey at the edges, and morning — and Professor Serin, and whatever paperwork disaster she'd prepared for me — was not nearly far enough away.
