The Depth of the Rot

1,059 words
Reading on

“Did something happen, Your Majesty?” Razakh, the governor of Tilmis, asked, his projection flickering on the intricately decorated crystal glass before the king.

Vazir crossed his fingers in front of his lips, took a deep breath, and prepared to deliver the news of his sister’s death.

“Ezra Ul Nazarana passed away today, past midnight,” he said in a low but clear voice.

The moment the words left his mouth, the faces of the four governors changed. He watched those changes closely, hoping to learn how deep the rot had spread.

Razakh Altarum, governor of Tilmis, frowned, his brows drawing together.

Panak Khulmann, governor of Baazar, looked stunned. His brows shot up and his jaw went slack.

Silvaris Donna, governor of Ganj, showed little change in her expression, but her eyes narrowed and her fists clenched.

Zalima Eklaire, governor of Somalis, remained quiet, which piqued his curiosity.

“How?” Panak broke the silence at once.

No one else spoke, urging Vazir to continue.

“We… don’t know for certain. But yesterday afternoon we happened to have some guests, you see…” He told them how the room where the two delegates of the Parshara Kingdom had stayed was found empty after the queen’s passing, and what condition the late queen’s body had been in.

“War! War it is!” Panak shot to his feet, his fists trembling, his voice breaking.

“Calm down, Panak,” Razakh said. “Nothing will be solved in anger. Your Highness, if you may…”

Razakh urged him to go on. He nodded.

“I understand what you feel, Panak. But there is something I must discuss with all of you in order to reach a reasonable conclusion.” He sat up straighter. “I think there is a hidden power manipulating this entire situation,” he said in one breath.

This time, all of them were at a loss for words.

“May I ask how Your Majesty reached such a conclusion?” Zalima asked, her usual nonchalance gone, her expression contorted.

“It’s… let’s just say it’s intuition,” Vazir replied vaguely.

He could see confusion and denial on every face, yet he could not tell them about the diary, not until he trusted someone enough.

“What do you want to propose?” This time it was Silvaris. She cut straight to the point, realizing he was beating around the bush.

He sighed. “I want the restrictions on the trade routes lifted, and…” He paused. “I want to resume importing glowing stones from the Parshara Kingdom, given the mounting cost of oil and other fuels.”

This time the reaction was explosive.

Panak, who had held himself in check until now, slammed his fist onto the table in front of him. A brown glow enveloped him, and the table shattered into fine pieces.

“An insult! This is an insult to the late crown!” he roared, his eyes bloodshot, his receding hairline in full display. A nasty scar ran diagonally down his face, making him look all the more intimidating.

“I will skin those bastards alive the moment I find them,” he declared, and with that, his projection ended.

Vazir drew a deep breath and kept his orange aura from flaring up. A surge of energy rose in his chest, then subsided.

He closed his eyes and let the silence stretch.

It’s not that I don’t want to give you proof. But I’m trapped. Everywhere I go in this castle, I can feel their gazes.

“He was presumptuous, yes, but he was not wrong. Your Majesty, if I may, I suggest you think it over alone.” With that, Silvaris’s projection vanished.

Only Zalima Eklaire and Razakh Altarum remained.

“Rest, Your Majesty,” Zalima said, and she too closed her projection.

Now he was alone with Razakh Altarum. He finally relaxed and spoke in a low voice, without any air of formality.

“How have you been, Grandpa?”

A smile blossomed on the old man’s aged face. “Just living… since your grandmother passed away,” he said, half in complaint.

Vazir smiled bitterly and told him about his life so far in the Nerith Kingdom, where he had studied at the Imperial Academy. Razakh listened intently, reacting to every part of the story. When he ran out of tales, he blurted out, almost unconsciously:

“If I had arrived sooner, could I have saved her?” He had spoken his thoughts aloud.

Razakh’s eyes softened. “What happened was not in your hands, so it should not weigh on you,” he said warmly. He added, as if reminiscing, “Life is like that. You never know when you may lose someone, which is why we should cherish every moment.”

After that, Razakh excused himself and shut off the projection.

“Ow!” Vazir looked down at his thumb, which had turned red. He realized he had been rubbing his hand with it for nearly the whole conversation.

He looked around and noticed the room was brighter than when the meeting began. Dawn had broken. Sunlight fell through the windows, unable to be held back by the red silk curtains.

His gaze moved to the long table before him. It was made of gold and stretched from one wall to the other. Black obsidian chairs lined its edges, reflecting the sunlight and giving them a twinkling sheen.

“It’s unlike you to think so much.”

He heard a familiar voice.

“Big Sister?” At the far end of the table sat a woman with flowing black hair, lavender eyes like his own, and a face that almost matched his.

She smiled. “I would rather start throwing punches than go through the tedious process of planning. Isn’t that what you are like?”

He rubbed his eyes. She was gone. There was no one in the room.

Heh… hehe… hahaha. He began to laugh. His lips curled into a smile, yet tears flowed freely from his eyes, staining the table below.

“You really know me inside out, Big Sister,” Vazir Ul Nazarana muttered in a trance.

Only now did it truly strike him: You have left me…
