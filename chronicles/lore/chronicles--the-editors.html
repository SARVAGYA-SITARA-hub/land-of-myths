The Editors

status: proposed — imported from the master bible. Almost entirely unestablished in the published text.

Definition

The authorities who review the manuscript. Not gods — gods are contents of the manuscript. The Editors stand outside it, deciding whether it stands.

Purpose

Why does the manuscript require review? Unresolved. Two candidates:

  • Quality control. The multiverse is finite in some resource — attention, belief, stability — and worlds compete for it. Review is triage.
  • Authorship. Someone wants to write, and every existing world is in the way. Review is a means of opening a slot.

The second is darker and fits the published tone better. PROPOSED, not decided.

Authority

  • Can: approve, reject, revise, reassign, erase.
  • Cannot (proposed): create from nothing. An Editor edits; it does not author. That limitation is Arin's opening — an Editor needs material, and Arin supplies forgeries.
  • Bound by: Author Candidacy.

Relationship with reality

They are not in the multiverse. They relate to it as a reader to a page: total access, no presence. They cannot be fought — only satisfied, evaded, or replaced.

Internal structure

  • Known Editors: none named.
  • Roles: unresolved.
  • Conflicts: the bible's own implied tension is that different Editors hold different standards.
  • Unknown members: implied.

Relationship with Arin

  • Initial: he does not know they exist. He thinks he is cheating a world.
  • Later: he learns the world was already flagged, and his forgeries are either the evidence against it or its defence.
  • Final: unresolved. The published premise — the world forgets him while belief keeps him alive — suggests he ends outside the review rather than passing it.

Narrative function

The question they ask is the book's question: who has the right to say what a world is? Arin's answer is "whoever can make people believe it." That is either the truth about authorship or the crime of it, and the novel should not settle which until late.

Open questions

  • Are the Editors people, systems, or a role anyone can be promoted into?
  • Is one of them already inside Arin's story under another name? Nothing confirms it.
  • Do they know Arin is forging — and is passing review worse than failing?
