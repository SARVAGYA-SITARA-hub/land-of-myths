Arin Valen

status: proposed — assembled from the published text (207 mentions) and the master bible.

Established by the published chapters

  • Protagonist. POV is first person, marked {Arin's POV}.
  • Age 13, becoming 14 through the published run.
  • Reincarnated. The story opens in a life that is over; he wakes into a world that is "not exactly the same" Earth.
  • Family: had a brother and a father ("Father" appears often, warm). Ezra is a close figure whose death drives the current arc — 15 mentions.
  • Hayato — the closest companion, 110 mentions; a Japanese-named boy, village near Somalis; last published chapter has Arin thinking of "Hayato-san in a village near Somalis, of a fire I would not be able to light."
  • Rhen — a name Arin does not consider himself to be: "Not soldier. Not Rhen."
  • Career premise: the early chapters are an examination — aptitude, reasoning, current affairs, ethics, Fog Phenomenology. He is being recruited by inter-world institutions.
  • Wealth/status: poor enough that the "Labour Exchange Program" and unpaid labour arc matter; he works.

Voice

First person, dry, self-deprecating, fluent in the language of an exam he is failing. Casual profanity. He narrates his own humiliations without flinching and without self-pity — "I lay on my back in the dirt... and tried very hard to look like a man who had been kicked off a griffin."

That is unusually good comic register for the genre and should be preserved absolutely.

Inheritance and power

Undivine, External, Spiritual Energy of Shunya → Mythology and Rumor (as the False Prophet's mirror).

Not yet confirmed on the page whether the reader has seen Mythologise used. UNRESOLVED — check before writing ch 56.

The path, per the master bible

  1. Mythology — invent a myth.
  2. Rumour — spread it.
  3. Belief — it becomes real, and he can use what it makes real.

Costs: justification (the lie must be argued for), belief (finite fuel), and fragmentation (each belief splits him further from himself). See Personality Erosion.

His central irony

He is a forgery competing with an absence. The False Prophet grows by being mistaken for Arin, using no effort at all — it simply is believed. Arin must work for every gram of belief he gets, and the impostor gets it free.

  • Is his family alive in this world, or is each one a different version?
  • What exactly did he lose to the Undivine path so far? The price should be visible by ch 55.
