Hayato

status: proposed — assembled from the published text (110 mentions, second-most-cited character). Healthier ground than Arin's sheet: most of this is on the page.

Established

  • Japanese-named; Arin calls him Hayato-san — respect, and a slightly older register.
  • From a village near Somalis. The newest published chapter has Arin thinking of "Hayato-san in a village near Somalis, of a fire I would not be able to light" — so Hayato is away from home, and Arin owes him something he cannot repay.
  • Currently travelling with Arin, fugitives together. They ride horses; Hayato unties them.
  • He is a mentor-shaped peer. His dialogue is the book's clearest thinking:

"…ally distinguish between front line and negotiating table." "He knows he is not a spear. He is not trying to pretend otherwise. That already puts him above—"

"Those who lose their script are often the only honest ones you get." "He did something rare for his age group. He admitted he needed to reset instead of faking it."

Why he works

Hayato is the only character who talks about honesty as a technique. In a novel where the protagonist's power is literally forgery, the deuteragonist's virtue is admitting you have lost your place. That is a deliberate and well-built contrast — protect it.

He is also the character who names what Arin is doing wrong without being an antagonist. That is rare and valuable.

Open questions

  • How did they meet? During the examination, or after?
  • Is he human? Nothing suggests otherwise, but nothing confirms it either.
  • Does he know about Arin's pathway?
