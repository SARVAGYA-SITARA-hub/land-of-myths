Against the Heavens · Chapter 11

The Young Master Has Become a Hermit

1,980 words
Reading on

The Wilhelmshire estate had a peculiar problem.

Its second young master had become difficult to find.

Not because he had left the estate.

Quite the opposite.

Ajax Wilhelmshire was almost always in his room.

He read there, rested there, ate there, and occasionally requested that certain books be brought to him. The servants assigned to his floor had become accustomed to carrying trays of food upstairs, collecting empty dishes, and delivering fresh stacks of paper.

At first, nobody had considered it particularly unusual.

Young Master Ajax had always been fond of books. His departure for the Central Academy was approaching, and perhaps he was simply preparing himself.

But a few days became a week.

Then another.

And gradually, the household began to notice.

*

"Has Young Master Ajax eaten?"

The question came from the lady of the house.

A servant standing near the dining room entrance bowed.

"Yes, Madam. His meal was delivered to his room a little while ago."

"Again?"

The servant hesitated.

"Yes, Madam."

The lady glanced at the empty chair beside Elena.

Her son had not joined them for dinner in several days.

"Did he say why?"

"He mentioned that he was reading, Madam."

Elena placed her spoon down.

"He's always reading."

Her mother looked at her.

"That is hardly a crime."

"I didn't say it was."

Elena folded her arms.

"He doesn't even come out to talk anymore. Yesterday, I went to his room, and he told me he was busy."

"What was he doing?"

"Reading."

"Then perhaps he was busy."

"He wasn't even turning the pages!"

Her mother paused.

"...What?"

"He was staring at the same page. I waited for almost five minutes."

Elena looked genuinely offended.

"I think he was pretending."

A faint smile appeared on her mother's face.

"Perhaps he was thinking."

"About what? The page?"

"Elena."

"Fine."

She returned to her food, though her expression remained dissatisfied.

The lady of the house looked towards the empty chair again.

Ajax had never been particularly sociable, but he had always made an effort to join family meals. He listened to their conversations, occasionally exchanged remarks with his father, and endured Elena's endless complaints with remarkable patience.

Now, he seemed content to remain in his room.

She did not wish to force him out.

Still, she intended to speak with him.

*

The following afternoon, his father visited.

Ajax was seated beside the window, a book resting on his lap.

His private diary was safely tucked away in a drawer.

He looked up when the door opened.

"Father."

"Am I interrupting?"

"Not particularly."

His father glanced at the book.

"That is reassuring. I was beginning to suspect that I needed an appointment to speak with my own son."

Ajax considered that.

"I could ask the servants to prepare a schedule."

"Please don't."

His father pulled a chair closer and sat down.

For a moment, he simply observed his son.

Ajax appeared well enough. His complexion was normal, and there was no obvious sign of illness. He did seem somewhat more tired than usual, although that might have been the result of spending too much time indoors.

"Your mother is concerned."

Ajax looked up.

"Is something wrong?"

"That is what she would like to know."

"With me?"

"Unless someone else has been secretly occupying your room."

Ajax glanced around.

"I would have noticed."

His father sighed.

"Ajax."

"I am listening."

"Why have you stopped joining us for meals?"

Ajax thought about it.

There was no particularly complicated explanation.

He had found that practising in his room was more convenient. Going downstairs, eating with everyone, and returning afterwards interrupted his routine.

Besides, the sensation had begun appearing during ordinary activities. It was easier to observe when he did not have to divide his attention between conversations.

"I have been occupied with my studies."

"Your academy studies?"

"Among other things."

His father raised an eyebrow.

"Among other things?"

Ajax realised he had perhaps answered too quickly.

"Historical research."

That was not entirely untrue.

His father looked at him for a moment before leaning back.

"You know, there is no requirement for you to finish the entire history of the world before entering the academy."

"I shall try to restrain myself."

His father shook his head, amused despite himself.

Then his expression became a little more serious.

"Your mother worries that you are isolating yourself."

Ajax looked towards the window.

He had not particularly thought about it.

He was still in the same house. He spoke to the servants, occasionally received Elena's visits, and met his parents whenever they came to see him.

It had simply become less frequent.

"I don't intend to avoid the family."

"Then perhaps you should occasionally demonstrate that."

Ajax looked back at him.

There was no accusation in his father's voice.

Only a quiet suggestion.

"I understand."

His father stood.

"Good. Your mother would appreciate seeing you at dinner."

Ajax hesitated.

"Tonight?"

"Unless you have arranged an appointment with your book."

"I shall consider cancelling it."

His father gave him a look.

"See that you do."

*

Ajax did not attend dinner that evening.

He had intended to.

He genuinely had.

But an observation during his afternoon meditation had led to another experiment, which had produced an unexpected result. By the time he finished recording it, dinner had already been served.

He stared at the clock.

Then at his diary.

"...That was unfortunate."

He could hear Elena's voice in his imagination.

He's always reading.

Ajax closed the diary.

Perhaps tomorrow.

*

Meanwhile, outside the Wilhelmshire estate, the young master's behaviour had become a subject of conversation.

The first rumours were relatively harmless.

At a small gathering hosted by a noble family, two ladies discussed the upcoming academy term while their attendants arranged refreshments.

"Have you heard? Young Master Wilhelmshire has apparently become quite the scholar."

"Ajax?"

"Yes. I was told he spends nearly all his time reading."

"Well, he is going to the Central Academy. Perhaps he is preparing."

"Perhaps. Though I heard he has stopped attending family dinners."

The other lady raised her eyebrows.

"Really?"

"Apparently, even his meals are sent to his room."

"That sounds rather excessive."

"Or perhaps he simply dislikes company."

They exchanged a brief glance.

Neither seemed particularly concerned.

The conversation soon moved to the academy's upcoming term and the latest political appointments.

But the rumour did not disappear.

It merely travelled.

*

A few days later, the interpretation had changed.

At a gathering attended by several upper-class families, a gentleman mentioned the matter while discussing the Wilhelmshire household.

"I hear their second young master has become something of a recluse."

"Recluse?"

"Apparently, he rarely leaves his room."

"Is he unwell?"

"I couldn't say. There are rumours that his health has deteriorated."

Another man took a sip from his glass.

"Wouldn't the family announce something if that were the case?"

"Not necessarily. The Wilhelmshires have always been rather private about family matters."

A third man, who had been listening quietly, joined the conversation.

"I heard something different. Apparently, the boy has developed an obsession with ancient records."

"An obsession?"

"Old manuscripts, historical documents, forgotten civilisations. That sort of thing."

The first gentleman laughed.

"Then perhaps he intends to become an archaeologist."

"At his age?"

"Why not?"

The third man shrugged.

"Who knows? Young people have strange interests."

The conversation continued.

Nobody had any real information.

That did not prevent them from discussing it.

*

Not every interpretation was charitable.

A few days later, the matter reached a more political gathering.

One of the attendees had recently been refused an invitation to meet the young Wilhelmshire.

"I was told he was unavailable."

"Perhaps he was occupied."

"For several days?"

The gentleman gave a dismissive laugh.

"An interesting way to treat an invitation."

A woman sitting nearby turned towards him.

"Perhaps the young man is unwell."

"Then his family should say so."

"Would you prefer them to publish a medical report?"

A few people chuckled.

The gentleman did not.

"Regardless, it is rather strange. The family is preparing him for the academy, yet he seems unwilling to participate in even the simplest social gatherings."

"Perhaps he doesn't care for them."

"That would be an unfortunate trait for someone of his family."

The woman regarded him over her cup.

"Or perhaps he is simply not interested in entertaining you."

The conversation shifted shortly afterwards.

But the remark remained.

And so did the rumour.

Some called him eccentric.

Others called him arrogant.

A few suggested that the Wilhelmshires were concealing a health problem.

There were even those who wondered whether his withdrawal had something to do with internal family affairs.

None of them knew the truth.

And, as was often the case, the truth was considerably less interesting to them than the possibilities.

*

Back at the estate, the household had reached its own conclusions.

Elena had decided that her brother was becoming a hermit.

The servants believed he was preparing for the academy with unusual dedication.

His mother was concerned that he was spending too much time alone.

His father was beginning to suspect that the books had become a more demanding companion than the rest of the family.

Ajax, meanwhile, was unaware of most of these developments.

He was seated at his desk, examining a page of handwritten observations.

A small plate of food rested beside him.

He had already finished eating.

His attention was on the faint sensation he had gradually become accustomed to perceiving.

It was there.

Quiet.

Familiar.

He did not need to close his eyes or regulate his breathing to recognise it anymore.

He simply continued reading.

A knock came at the door.

"Young Master?"

"Come in."

A servant entered carrying a fresh tray.

"Madam asked me to remind you that the family will be having breakfast together tomorrow."

Ajax looked up.

"Breakfast?"

"Yes, Young Master."

He glanced at the clock.

Then at the servant.

"Please inform her that I will attend."

The servant bowed.

"Certainly, Young Master."

As the door closed, Ajax returned to his notes.

He had no particular objection to breakfast.

In fact, he rather enjoyed his mother's cooking.

He would simply have to finish his experiment earlier.

*

The following morning, Elena arrived at the dining room before everyone else.

She took her usual seat and waited.

Her brother arrived a few minutes later.

She stared at him.

Ajax looked at her.

"Good morning."

Elena continued staring.

"...You're here."

"I am."

"Actually here."

"I can see that."

She looked towards their mother, who was trying rather unsuccessfully to hide her amusement.

Ajax took his seat.

His father arrived shortly afterwards.

For a while, breakfast proceeded normally.

Elena spoke about the clothes she intended to take to the academy. Their mother asked Ajax whether he had finished preparing his belongings. Their father mentioned a few matters concerning the upcoming departure.

Ajax answered when spoken to.

He even joined the conversation occasionally.

Nothing extraordinary happened.

Yet the atmosphere seemed a little lighter.

At one point, Elena leaned towards him.

"Are you coming to dinner too?"

Ajax considered the question.

"I shall try."

"That's what you said last time."

"Then I shall try more successfully."

Elena looked unconvinced.

His father laughed.

His mother smiled.

And for a little while, the second young master of the Wilhelmshire household was no longer a rumour, a recluse, or an eccentric scholar.

He was simply their son and brother.

*

That evening, however, Ajax returned to his room.

His private diary lay open on the desk.

He had several observations to record, and the new book he had requested from the estate library had finally arrived.

He sat down.

Outside, the orchid trees swayed in the evening breeze.

Inside, the faint sensation continued to flow through his awareness.

Ajax picked up his pen.

There was still much to learn.

And somewhere beyond the walls of the estate, people continued to discuss the peculiar habits of the young Wilhelmshire.
