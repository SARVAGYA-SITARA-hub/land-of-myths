The Diary

1,889 words
Reading on

The guests started to arrive.

By then, the main hall had transformed.

The flowers were in place, the silverware had been inspected twice, and the musicians had begun playing a gentle tune near the western balcony on the raised platform. The enormous entrance doors remained open, allowing servants to welcome the guests as carriages arrived one after another.

Ajax had been positioned near the entrance.

Not officially, of course.

His mother had simply suggested that he might enjoy greeting some of the guests. His father had agreed. And since refusing would only invite another discussion about his health, Ajax had accepted.

He sat beside a small decorative table, dressed in a dark formal coat with silver buttons. His thick black hair swept back and his obsidian eyes set on the entrance.

His wheelchair had been polished that afternoon.

He suspected someone had spent more time polishing it than his shoes.

“Welcome, Sir Hemingway.”

Ajax inclined his head as an elderly man approached.

“Ah! Young Ajax. You've grown up.”

Ajax had heard that sentence at least a hundred times.

“I'm afraid I haven't had much of a choice.”

The man chuckled politely.

His wife offered him a smile.

“How are you feeling these days?”

“Quite well, thank you.”

“That's wonderful. Your parents must be very relieved.”

“They are.”

The conversation lasted another minute before the couple moved towards the main hall.

Ajax watched them leave.

The smile had been pleasant enough. It simply hadn't reached their eyes.

The next guest was a merchant whose company had recently secured a trade agreement with the royal family. He spoke to Ajax for nearly five minutes, though most of those minutes were spent discussing his father's recent economic reforms.

A military officer came next.

Then a representative of the royal family. Then a distant relative.

He answered everyone.

He remembered names, exchanged greetings, and occasionally made a joke when the opportunity presented itself.

Most people responded kindly.

Some even seemed genuinely pleased to see him.

But there was always a certain hesitation.

They avoided asking about his future. And whenever his wheelchair became the subject of conversation, their expressions acquired that particular softness reserved for unfortunate things.

Ajax was familiar with it.

He didn't particularly dislike these people. Most were simply uncomfortable.

And he had no intention of making them more uncomfortable.

Once the initial wave of guests had arrived, he quietly moved towards the side of the hall.

*

The Wilhelmshire family occupied a peculiar position in the republic.

The country had a royal family, and the crown remained the highest authority. The governors were appointed to oversee the major administrative territories, managing their affairs, maintaining institutions, and implementing royal policy.

There were five governors in total.

They were not nobles in the traditional sense.

They did not own the lands they administered, nor could they treat their territories as personal inheritances. Their authority came from the crown, and their responsibilities extended only as far as the royal administration permitted.

They were, in a sense, limbs of the royal government.

A healthy kingdom needed functioning limbs.

But they were still limbs.

The governor of a territory could command considerable influence, control administrative appointments, negotiate trade arrangements, and oversee regional security.

Naturally, the position came with considerable privileges.

The Wilhelmshires were wealthy, influential, and counted among the republic's most prominent families. Their residence was large enough to host hundreds of guests, and their social circle included big names and top shots from various countries.

They were the so called upper class.

Ajax understood that his father's position was one reason people were so interested in speaking with him.

The other reason was considerably less flattering.

A little curiosity, a little sympathy, and occasionally a little gossip.

*

He was watching a group of musicians when he spotted a familiar face.

Brown hair.

Green eyes.

A crooked collar.

Zhen Shuri.

The boy was carrying a tray of glasses near the far end of the hall.

He looked different from earlier.

His movements were slower, and his eyes seemed to wander without settling on anything for too long. There were dark circles beneath them, and his hair had become noticeably messier.

He nearly walked into another servant.

“Careful!”

“Sorry.”

Zhen adjusted the tray and continued.

Ajax watched him for a moment.

The boy who was fine a moment ago, now looked as though he hadn't slept properly in days.

Ajax tilted his head.

Interesting. He thought

The boy who had spent five minutes polishing a painted spot seemed to have lost the energy to argue with the world.

*

“Ajax.”

His father's voice came from behind him.

He turned.

Governor Wilhelmshire stood beside him, accompanied by a middle-aged man wearing a dark green suit and a woman with silver ornaments in her hair.

“Come. I'd like you to meet someone.”

Ajax straightened slightly

“This is chancellor Matthew Ornavis, his wife, and their daughter.”

Ajax greeted them.

“It's an honor to meet you, Sir Matthew.” Ajax tried to kneel but was abruptly stopped by the chancellor.

“No, no what are you doing kid” he reprimanded Ajax while simultaneously setting him back in his wheelchair. “I've heard quite a bit about you.” He said smiling warmly at him.

Ajax wondered what exactly he had heard.

“Your father speaks highly of you,” the minister continued.

He raised an eyebrow turning towards his father, who in turn gave him a brief look and then coughed.

Seeing that Sir Matthew laughed.

“Ha! You have a sense of humour. Good. That's always useful in politics.”

“I've heard it can be fatal in certain times.”

This time, even his wife smiled.

They moved towards a quieter seating area near the balcony. A servant brought refreshments, and the adults began discussing matters that Ajax had heard often enough around the house.

Ajax listened.

He wasn't expected to contribute, but he had always found these conversations interesting.

Ajax took a sip of water.

The discussion continued for several minutes before chancellor Matthew turned towards him.

“So, Have you decided what you intend to do in the future?”

Ajax lowered his glass.

“I haven't made a final decision.”

“Hmm, then I would but suggest to make that quickly” the minister said. “My daughter will be attending Mora’s Council of Magic in Lahir Empire, starting this winter.”

Ajax glanced towards the young girl who joined them a couple of moments ago.

She appeared to be around his age. She had long blonde hair and red eyes which calmly swept across the room.

“Mora’s Council of Magic?” Ajax asked again.

His father nodded.

“It's located in the South, Situated and founded in Lahir Empire.”

**“It’s the most prestigious institutions on the continent in terms of Magic.”**Ajax observed.

“Indeed,” Sir Matthew replied. “The Council accepts students from several countries. It has a particularly good reputation for magical sciences, though getting admission is extremely tough.”

Ajax nodded and fell into deep thought.

His father glanced at him and smiled.

“I have given Ajax’s application in Altos Institute of History and Literature sciences.”

Ajax blinked, surprised. Sir Matthew smiled and for some reason “My, my look at the time. Now if you would excuse us” sprinted off with his family in tow.

Ajax looked at them leaving and then, turned towards his father.

“What about little Rose?” he asked his father about his little sister.

“I plan to teach her myself.” His father said.

Ajax considered for a moment. He knew that his father had long decided to nurture his sister as his successor. It was not due to his biases but the fact that his sister had a natural talent in politics and negotiations. She could be said an ideal candidate.

That was that but, he also knew that she would never accept the position due to Ajax himself and so this can also be said an ideal situation where he would also get to explore the world and she would learn by his father’s side.

He wondered whether she knew such a side of father.

“I don’t know if I am making the right choice” his father finally spoke, not matching him in the eyes.

“But I want the best for both of you.” He stroked Ajax’s head affectionately and left to wrap up the remaining events.

Ajax saw his father leave. The man who he had seen always confident and fearless of his choices , for the first time hesitated to make a decision. That made Ajax realize that his father was also just a human.

He wasn't sure whether being sent to a foreign academy counted as a childhood experience, but he kept the thought to himself.

At last he could only sigh as the world around him continued to move.

*

When the last guests finally departed, the mansion descended into a different kind of chaos.

Servants began clearing tables, collecting decorations, and restoring the hall to its usual appearance.

The musicians packed their instruments.

The guards resumed their regular patrols.

And the birthday girl, having exhausted the majority of her energy, was carried upstairs by their mother.

The family gathered in the drawing room afterwards.

His father had removed his formal coat, while his mother sat with a cup of tea.

Ajax occupied his usual place near the window.

“Did you enjoy yourself?” his mother asked.

“Very much.” Ajax replied.

“Good” She seemed satisfied.

His father took a deep breath and looked at him. He nodded back and then his father told his mother that he plans to send Ajax to Altos Institute of History and Literature sciences. His mother immediately started firing arguments against it and it took a long time to finally convince her.

In the process Ajax himself learnt many new things as his father continued explaining the academy, the travel arrangements, and the courses available to them.

Ajax listened.

He asked a few questions about the institution's library, its department, and the facilities available to students with physical disabilities.

His father answered what he could.

Some details would need to be confirmed with the institute itself.

After the discussion was over Ajax was escorted back to his room.

The day had been long.

He was tired.

After changing into his nightclothes, he settled into bed and stared at the ceiling.

Ajax wondered why he looked so exhausted.

He closed his eyes.

There would be time to think about it tomorrow.

*

He opened his eyes.

The ceiling was gone.

So was his bed.

Ajax found himself suspended in an endless expanse of darkness.

Except it wasn't entirely dark.

Stars stretched in every direction, countless points of light scattered across an immeasurable black sky.

There was no ground beneath him.

No walls.

No sound.

He looked down.

His wheelchair was gone.

His legs were there, or at least he could feel them, but the sensation was strangely distant.

Ajax remained still.

He tried to move his fingers.

They responded.

He looked around again.

“Am I dreaming?”

No answer.

Then something appeared before him.

A translucent window.

It hovered in the starry expanse, its surface faintly illuminated by a pale silver light.

Letters began forming across it.

[Extracting Inheritance………]

Ajax stared.

A second line appeared.

[Dual Nurture Program- Phase - 1]

The window remained suspended before him.

Ajax continued staring at the words.

After a while, he raised an eyebrow.

“...What?”
