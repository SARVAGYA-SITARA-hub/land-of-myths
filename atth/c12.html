The Crown's Reception

3,506 words
Reading on

The invitation arrived three days after the Wilhelmshire family had resumed having breakfast together.

It was delivered in a dark blue envelope, bearing the royal crest in silver.

Ajax noticed it before anyone else.

"That looks expensive."

His father, who had been reading the accompanying letter, glanced at him.

"It is an invitation."

"I gathered that much from the envelope."

His mother gave him a warning look.

Ajax returned to his breakfast.

Elena, however, had already abandoned her food.

"Is it from the palace?"

"Yes," her father replied.

"Are we invited?"

"We are."

"All of us?"

"Yes, Elena."

She sat straighter.

"What's happening?"

Her father folded the letter.

"The Crown is holding its annual reception for the departing generation."

Ajax looked up.

"The departing generation?"

"Those who will be entering academies, military institutions, research establishments and other professional institutes this coming term. The Crown hosts a gathering before they disperse."

His mother added, "It gives the young people an opportunity to meet one another. Many of them will spend years in different institutions and countries. It is easier to establish connections before everyone goes their separate ways."

"And their families," his father continued. "Naturally, the adults have their own reasons for attending."

Ajax nodded.

"Political connections."

"Among other things."

"Trade agreements?"

"Sometimes."

"Military appointments?"

His father smiled.

"Occasionally."

"Marriage arrangements?"

His mother nearly dropped her cup.

"Ajax."

"What? It seemed like a reasonable possibility."

Elena giggled.

His father coughed into his hand.

"Your mother would appreciate it if you didn't make every conversation sound like a parliamentary investigation."

"I'll endeavour to improve."

Elena leaned forward.

"Will there be people from the Central Academy?"

"Certainly."

"People our age?"

"That's rather the point."

"Will there be food?"

Ajax looked at his sister.

"That was rather predictable."

Elena ignored him.

His mother smiled.

"There will be a formal dinner as well."

"Then I'm definitely going."

"That was never in question," her father said.

Ajax looked back at the invitation.

He had spent the past few weeks avoiding unnecessary social gatherings. His parents had been patient about it, though he suspected that patience was beginning to wear thin.

Still, an invitation from the Crown was not something one casually declined.

"When is it?"

"Tomorrow evening."

Ajax sighed internally.

Apparently, his books would have to tolerate some competition.

*

The following evening, the royal palace was illuminated from the entrance courtyard to the eastern galleries.

The Crown's reception was not quite a ball, nor was it an ordinary dinner.

It was something between the two.

The main gallery had been opened to accommodate the guests. Long tables were arranged along the outer walls, while smaller seating arrangements occupied the central hall. Musicians played near the balconies, and servants moved between the guests with trays of refreshments.

The palace's architecture was impressive without being excessively decorative.

High ceilings rested upon broad pillars. The walls were covered with paintings depicting important events in the country's history, and large windows overlooked a garden filled with pale flowers.

At the far end of the gallery stood a raised platform bearing the royal crest.

There was no throne.

This was not a coronation or a formal audience.

It was a reception.

And the Crown wanted its guests to feel comfortable enough to speak.

Ajax entered alongside his parents and Elena.

His wheelchair moved quietly across the polished floor.

He glanced around.

There were considerably more people than he had expected.

Ministers, governors, military officers, industrialists, scholars, representatives of the royal court, and their families.

Some had travelled from distant administrative territories.

Others had arrived from foreign countries.

The younger guests were particularly easy to identify.

They stood in small groups, usually accompanied by someone older, and attempted to appear comfortable while discreetly observing everyone around them.

Some already knew one another.

Others were clearly trying to determine whom they should approach first.

Ajax found the arrangement mildly amusing.

A gathering intended to establish friendships had apparently begun with everyone calculating the usefulness of potential friends.

"Ajax."

He turned towards his mother.

"Yes?"

"Your father and I will be speaking with the Royal Secretary. Elena will remain with the other young ladies for a while."

Elena immediately looked offended.

"I can speak for myself."

"Nobody suggested otherwise."

"Then why did you say it like that?"

"Because I know you."

Ajax looked between them.

"Should I be concerned?"

His mother smiled.

"Only if your sister decides to negotiate."

Elena raised her chin.

"I negotiate very well."

"I've seen you negotiate for an additional dessert," Ajax replied.

"That was successful."

"Unfortunately."

His father laughed.

"Come. Let's greet the Secretary."

*

The first few minutes were spent exchanging formal greetings.

Ajax recognised several faces from earlier gatherings, including Minister Edric Voss.

The minister approached with his wife and daughter.

"Governor Wilhelmshire."

"Minister Voss."

The two men exchanged greetings before Edric turned towards Ajax.

"Young Ajax. It's good to see you again."

"The pleasure is mine, Minister."

"I hope you've been keeping well."

"Quite well, thank you."

Edric nodded.

Then he turned slightly.

"Allow me to introduce my daughter properly this time. Liora."

The young woman beside him inclined her head.

She had dark, neatly arranged hair and a composed expression. Her dress was elegant without being overly elaborate, and the small silver ornament pinned near her collar bore the insignia of the Voss family.

Ajax recognised her from the earlier banquet.

"It's a pleasure to meet you again," she said.

"Likewise."

"Father tells me you've chosen Archaeology and Literature."

"I am considering them."

"Considering?"

"I haven't decided whether I should study both or focus on one."

Liora smiled.

"That sounds like a difficult decision."

"Not particularly. I simply haven't found a convincing reason to choose."

"Then perhaps you should attend the Academy first."

"That is the current plan."

She laughed softly.

Edric looked pleased.

"My daughter has been interested in political history for years. She intends to study Political Studies and Historical Administration."

Ajax glanced at her.

"Historical Administration?"

"It's a relatively new programme," Liora explained. "It studies the development of administrative institutions, their records, and the relationship between political authority and historical documentation."

"That sounds interesting."

"It is. Although Father thinks I should have chosen something more practical."

Edric raised an eyebrow.

"I never said that."

"You suggested Economics."

"Because it is useful."

"Everything is useful to you if it can be included in a budget."

Ajax considered that.

"That sounds familiar."

Edric looked at him.

"Your father?"

Ajax nodded.

"Though I suspect the Royal Treasury has influenced him."

Governor Wilhelmshire laughed.

"Careful, son. The Treasury has ears."

Liora looked towards the raised platform.

"Do you think the Crown's reception is actually useful?"

Edric answered before Ajax could.

"Of course it is. You will meet people who may become important in your professional life."

"That isn't what I meant."

"Then what did you mean?"

"Do you think people will remember one another after tonight?"

The question was simple, but Edric did not answer immediately.

Ajax looked at her.

Liora continued.

"Everyone here is being introduced to everyone else. Names, families, institutions, ambitions. But most of us will leave for different places. Some will enter military academies. Others will study magic, engineering, medicine, or politics. We may not meet again for years."

"That doesn't make the meeting useless," Ajax said.

She turned towards him.

"Why?"

"Because you don't need to maintain a relationship continuously for it to matter. Sometimes you only need to remember that someone exists."

Liora considered that.

"That's a rather practical answer."

"I've been told that practicality is useful."

"By your father?"

"Mostly."

Edric laughed.

"Your son is becoming dangerous, Governor."

"He's always been dangerous when allowed to speak."

Ajax looked at his father.

"I'll remember that."

*

The reception grew livelier as the evening progressed.

A group of young men stood near the western balcony, discussing their respective institutions.

One of them wore the uniform of a military preparatory school. Another had the insignia of an engineering institute.

Ajax was introduced to them by Liora.

The first was named Darius Renwick, the son of a regional military commander. He was broad-shouldered, confident, and spoke with the certainty of someone who had never had to doubt his own future.

The second, Lucien Ardent, came from a family involved in magical manufacturing. He was quieter, though his eyes brightened whenever the conversation turned towards technology.

"You're joining the Central Academy?" Darius asked.

"Yes."

"Which department?"

"Possibly Archaeology and Literature."

Darius blinked.

"Both?"

"I'm still deciding."

"Why not Magical Sciences? The Academy is famous for it."

Ajax looked at him.

"Are you joining the military?"

"Military Command Institute."

"Then why not study engineering?"

Darius frowned.

"Because I'm joining the military."

"Exactly."

Lucien laughed.

Darius looked between them.

"What's so funny?"

"Nothing," Ajax replied. "I was simply observing that we seem to have different ideas about specialisation."

Darius folded his arms.

"I don't see the point of studying things that won't help you."

"That depends on what you consider helpful."

"Can Archaeology help you win a battle?"

Ajax thought for a moment.

"Occasionally."

Darius raised an eyebrow.

"How?"

"By telling you where the previous army failed."

Lucien laughed again.

Darius looked unconvinced.

"That's not the same as fighting."

"No. But it might help you avoid fighting somewhere you shouldn't."

The young military student considered that.

"You're saying history teaches strategy?"

"Among other things."

"Then why not study military history?"

"Perhaps I will."

Darius shook his head, though his expression had softened.

"You're difficult to argue with."

"I've been told."

Liora took a sip from her glass.

"That seems to be a recurring problem."

*

A little later, the group was approached by an older gentleman wearing a dark military uniform.

His hair was mostly grey, and several decorations were pinned across his chest.

Darius straightened immediately.

"General Halden."

The general nodded.

"Renwick."

He looked at Lucien, then at Liora.

Finally, his gaze settled on Ajax.

"Governor Wilhelmshire's son?"

Ajax inclined his head.

"Yes, General."

"I remember your father. We worked together during the northern security reforms."

Governor Wilhelmshire, who had been speaking with another minister nearby, noticed the general and approached.

"General Halden. It's been some time."

"Too long."

They exchanged greetings.

The general then looked towards the younger group.

"So. The next generation."

Darius immediately began explaining his plans for the Military Command Institute.

The general listened without interruption.

When Darius finished, he asked:

"Why do you want to join the military?"

Darius answered confidently.

"To serve the country."

"That's a good answer."

The general waited.

Darius seemed uncertain whether he was expected to say more.

"Anything else?"

"I want to become a commander."

"Why?"

"To lead."

The general nodded slowly.

"Everyone wants to lead. Very few want to be responsible for the people who follow them."

Darius frowned.

"Isn't that the same thing?"

"No."

The general glanced towards the balcony.

"When you are responsible for a hundred soldiers, you make decisions that affect a hundred lives. When you command a thousand, your mistakes become larger. When you command ten thousand, even your good intentions can become disasters."

The young man fell silent.

The general looked at Ajax.

"And you? What do you intend to become?"

"I haven't decided."

"That is an honest answer."

"I've found that making decisions before understanding the available choices is usually inefficient."

The general laughed.

"Your father has raised a cautious son."

Governor Wilhelmshire smiled.

"Only in certain matters."

"Then perhaps he has failed."

Ajax looked at the general.

"Is that a military assessment?"

"An old man's opinion."

"Those are often more difficult to challenge."

The general laughed again.

He pulled a chair closer and sat with the younger guests.

"Tell me something. What do you think makes a good commander?"

Darius answered first.

"Strength."

Lucien said, "Knowledge."

Liora considered it.

"Judgement."

Ajax remained quiet.

The general looked at him.

"And you?"

Ajax took a moment.

"Knowing when not to give an order."

The general's expression changed slightly.

"Explain."

"A commander may possess strength, knowledge and judgement. But if he believes every situation requires his intervention, he will eventually become the greatest obstacle to his own people."

The general watched him for several seconds.

Then he nodded.

"That's a better answer than most officers give me."

Darius looked mildly dissatisfied.

The general noticed.

"Don't misunderstand me, Renwick. Strength matters. Knowledge matters. Judgement matters. But you must learn to recognise the limits of your own judgement."

He leaned back.

"An army does not fail only because its commander is weak. Sometimes it fails because the commander refuses to listen."

Lucien nodded thoughtfully.

Liora looked at Ajax.

"That sounds like something worth remembering."

"It probably is."

The general chuckled.

"Good. At least one of you is listening."

*

Near the centre of the hall, a woman in a deep blue evening dress was speaking with several young guests.

She was introduced as Lady Yselle Maren, daughter of a senior naval administrator.

She was strikingly beautiful, with dark hair arranged in an intricate braid and a calm expression that made it difficult to determine whether she was amused or simply observant.

Unlike many of the other guests, she did not seem particularly interested in discussing family positions.

She was discussing ships.

More precisely, the lack of them.

"The northern ports are expanding," she said. "But the fleet is still relying on vessels designed for older trade routes."

Lucien looked interested.

"Are the new engines not sufficient?"

"Not entirely. The engines are better. The hull designs are not."

Darius frowned.

"Isn't that a naval matter?"

"Everything is a naval matter until it becomes an economic matter."

She turned towards Ajax.

"And you? What will you study?"

"Archaeology and Literature, perhaps."

"Then perhaps you can tell me why the Royal Maritime Archive has been neglecting its oldest records."

Ajax looked at her.

"That is a rather specific question."

"I've been told that scholars enjoy specific questions."

"Some do."

"Do you?"

"Usually, when they come with documents."

She smiled.

"Then you may be disappointed. Most of the documents are incomplete."

"That doesn't necessarily make them useless."

"Why?"

"An incomplete record can still tell you what someone considered worth recording. Sometimes the omissions are as interesting as the contents."

Lady Yselle studied him for a moment.

"That's an unusual way of looking at archives."

"Is it?"

"Most people want to know what happened."

Ajax glanced at the paintings on the walls.

"I think it's equally important to know who wanted people to remember it."

The woman smiled more genuinely this time.

"Perhaps you'll be useful after all."

Liora looked between them.

"I thought you were going to ask him about the ships."

"I did. We arrived at history instead."

"That happens quite often around him," Liora said.

Ajax looked at her.

"I wasn't aware I had developed a reputation."

"Not yet."

*

The evening continued.

People moved between conversations, exchanged contact information, and made arrangements to meet again after the institutions opened.

Some connections were sincere.

Others were clearly calculated.

A young man from a wealthy industrial family offered Lucien access to a private workshop. A daughter of a military minister exchanged correspondence details with Darius. Liora spoke with several students who intended to study political sciences.

Ajax, meanwhile, found himself having more conversations than he had expected.

He spoke with a historian about the preservation of magical ruins.

He listened to a military officer discuss the difficulties of transporting supplies across mountainous territories.

He even found himself explaining to a young woman why he preferred studying old maps to reading contemporary travel guides.

"Old maps are inaccurate," she argued.

"Often."

"Then why study them?"

"Because they tell you what people believed the world looked like."

"That seems rather indirect."

"History usually is."

She laughed.

At some point, Elena appeared beside him.

She looked pleased with herself.

"Have you made any friends?"

Ajax glanced around.

"I've spoken to several people."

"That isn't what I asked."

"Then perhaps you should ask a more precise question."

Elena rolled her eyes.

"You're impossible."

"That seems to be the general consensus."

She leaned closer.

"I met a girl from the eastern territory. She's joining the Central Academy too."

"That's good."

"And she knows a lot about magical fashion."

Ajax stared at her.

"Magical fashion?"

"Yes."

"Is that a department?"

"No."

"Then what does she study?"

"Magical Sciences."

"Ah."

Elena narrowed her eyes.

"Don't sound so disappointed."

"I wasn't aware I had."

"You did."

Ajax decided not to argue.

His sister was clearly enjoying herself.

He would not ruin it.

The formal dinner began shortly afterwards.

The tables were arranged in several sections, with the most senior officials seated near the royal platform.

The younger guests were placed together in a large dining hall adjoining the main gallery.

Ajax was seated beside Liora and Lucien.

Darius sat opposite them.

The first courses were familiar.

Roasted vegetables, fresh bread, soups, and several dishes prepared with locally sourced ingredients.

The atmosphere remained lively.

Then the servers began bringing out the special seafood course.

It was an unusual selection.

The Crown had commissioned a shipment from the Veylan Maritime Exchange, a company operating along the southern coast. The order had been placed specifically for the reception, and the company had supplied a team of culinary specialists alongside the seafood.

The palace's own kitchen had handled the preparation under the supervision of the royal banquet staff.

The head chef had inspected the shipment when it arrived.

The seals were intact.

The accompanying documents were in order.

The ingredients appeared fresh.

There had been no reason to suspect anything unusual.

The special course included silver-shell oysters, deepwater prawns, and a delicate white-flesh fish found only in the southern waters.

A light sauce accompanied the dishes.

The smell was pleasant.

The presentation was beautiful.

Liora looked at the plate.

"Have you tried this before?"

Ajax examined it.

"No."

"Neither have I."

Lucien had already taken a bite.

"It's excellent."

Darius nodded.

"Better than the usual palace menu."

Ajax looked at him.

"That's a rather bold statement."

"Try it."

Ajax picked up his fork.

He tasted a small portion.

The flavour was delicate, with a slight sweetness and a freshness he wasn't accustomed to.

"It is quite good."

Liora smiled.

"You sound surprised."

"I had prepared a complaint."

"About the food?"

"About the expense."

She laughed.

Lucien leaned forward.

"Apparently, the Crown ordered the entire shipment specifically for tonight."

"From the southern coast?" Ajax asked.

"Yes. Veylan Maritime Exchange. They supplied their own specialists as well."

Darius nodded.

"My father mentioned the company. They have been expanding their trade routes."

Liora looked towards the adults' table.

"That explains the number of merchants here."

"Does it?" Ajax asked.

"Of course. The Crown's reception is an opportunity to establish connections. A company that supplies the royal household gains a certain amount of prestige."

Lucien added, "And if the reception is successful, the Crown may continue ordering from them."

Darius smiled.

"Then perhaps the company has already won."

Ajax looked down at his plate.

"That depends on the food."

Darius laughed.

"You're impossible."

"Apparently."

The conversation moved on.

Nobody noticed anything unusual about the meal.

Nobody questioned the ingredients.

Nobody had any reason to.

Later in the evening, the royal secretary addressed the guests on behalf of the Crown.

His speech was brief.

He welcomed the young entrants, wished them success in their respective institutions, and reminded them that the country would eventually depend upon the work they did.

He spoke of knowledge, responsibility, cooperation and the importance of maintaining connections beyond the boundaries of one's profession.

There was polite applause.

Afterwards, the reception gradually began to wind down.

The younger guests exchanged final greetings.

Some promised to meet again.

Others were already discussing travel arrangements.

General Halden approached Governor Wilhelmshire before the family departed.

"Your son is an interesting young man."

Governor Wilhelmshire glanced at Ajax.

"That is one way to describe him."

"He listens more than he speaks."

"Usually."

"Keep that habit alive."

The governor smiled.

"I'll try."

The general turned towards Ajax.

"Remember something, young man. Institutions will teach you many things. Some will be useful. Others will merely be required."

Ajax nodded.

"But don't let the institution decide what you are allowed to become."

The general placed a hand on his shoulder.

"Good luck at the Academy."

"Thank you, General."

The journey home was quiet.

Elena was tired.

Their mother was speaking with their father about the guests they had met.

Ajax sat beside the carriage window, watching the palace lights disappear into the distance.

He had enjoyed the evening more than expected.

There had been interesting people.

Interesting conversations.

And several questions he wanted to investigate later.

He glanced down at his hand.

The faint sensation of Qi remained familiar.

He could feel its subtle circulation, though he had no control over it.

He looked back outside.

Tomorrow, he would return to his books.

For now, that was enough.

The Crown's reception had ended.

And nobody in the palace had any reason to believe that the following morning would be different from any other.
