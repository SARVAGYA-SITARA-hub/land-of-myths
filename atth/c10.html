Against the Heavens · Chapter 10

Reverse Heaven Divine Scripture

1,022 words
Reading on

The book was heavier than it looked.

Ajax rested it upon the stone table and examined its cover.

Reverse Heaven Divine Scripture.

There were no decorative inscriptions, no elaborate symbols, and no explanation of its contents.

Just a title.

He opened the first page.

The writing was familiar.

The same strange script that somehow settled into his understanding whenever he read Cheonma's diary.

But unlike the earlier sections, this one began without an introduction.

Qi originates from Heaven.

It descends to Earth.

It returns to Heaven.

Thus, the cycle continues.

Ajax read the lines twice.

The next page contained a simple illustration.

A circular pattern, with Heaven at the top and Earth at the bottom. Several arrows connected the two, forming an uninterrupted cycle.

Below it were a few more sentences.

The cultivator follows this cycle.

The closer the mind approaches Heaven, the purer the Qi.

The purer the Qi, the greater its capacity.

The meridians expand. The dantian deepens.

Ajax leaned back slightly.

It was a familiar principle.

The cultivation traditions he had encountered in books often described something similar. The mind, through discipline and understanding, gradually aligned itself with the natural order.

But the next page was different.

There was only one question.

Why?

Ajax paused.

He turned the page.

Why should purity of mind determine the purity of Qi?

Another page.

Qi follows its nature.

The mind follows its nature.

Why must one depend upon the other?

Ajax's eyes narrowed.

He continued reading.

The following entries were brief.

Perhaps the mind is not merely following Qi.

Perhaps it changes what it receives.

He turned another page.

The self distinguishes.

The self interprets.

The self imposes.

What passes through it may no longer remain unchanged.

Ajax stopped.

He read the lines again.

Then slowly turned the page.

There was a rough diagram.

A natural stream of Qi entered a circle marked Mind. On the other side, the flow emerged differently.

The original illustration was incomplete. Several lines had been crossed out, and a few annotations had been added beside it.

One sentence remained beneath the diagram.

The mind may be a filter.

Ajax looked at it for a while.

"...Interesting."

He continued.

*

The next section contained the results of an experiment.

Or, more accurately, the beginning of one.

I observed the flow.

I observed the mind.

I observed the difference.

There were several pages of short notes, most of them concerning the relationship between the natural flow of Qi and the state of the cultivator's mind.

Some observations contradicted earlier assumptions.

Others ended with questions.

Then he found a passage that caught his attention.

The self is not empty.

It desires. It rejects. It distinguishes itself from the world.

Perhaps this is why the Qi changes.

Ajax read the lines carefully.

Cheonma had not written much.

But the implication was becoming clearer.

The mind was not necessarily purifying Qi by becoming one with Heaven.

Perhaps conventional cultivation purified Qi because the practitioner gradually abandoned the distinctions imposed by the self.

And perhaps the opposite was possible.

If the self could act as a filter, then strengthening its influence might produce the opposite result.

Not purity.

Something else.

He turned the page.

I attempted to impose the self upon the natural flow.

The Qi changed.

It did not become pure.

It became mine.

Ajax remained still.

The next few pages contained more diagrams.

The natural cycle appeared several times, but the arrows were different in each illustration. Some were crossed out. Others had been redrawn.

Cheonma seemed to be examining how the flow changed when subjected to the will of the cultivator.

There were no detailed instructions.

No elaborate explanation of the process.

Only a handful of observations.

Natural Qi follows Heaven.

The self imposes its order.

The flow is altered.

The result is Demonic Qi.

Ajax stared at the final line.

He turned another page.

It was nearly empty.

Only a short passage had been written near the bottom.

I have not understood the method.

I have only confirmed the possibility.

The self must become the source of the imposed order.

Until then, the change remains superficial.

Ajax looked back at the earlier diagrams.

A thought occurred to him.

If the mind was a filter, then the transformation was not simply a matter of directing Qi differently.

It was a matter of changing the principle through which Qi was received and circulated.

He did not yet understand how that could be achieved.

Neither, apparently, had Cheonma.

At least, not when he wrote these notes.

Ajax continued reading.

The final pages contained a heading.

Reverse Heaven Divine Scripture.

Beneath it was a short statement.

Heaven gives the order.

The self rejects it.

The Qi changes.

There was no further explanation.

Ajax closed the book.

For a while, he remained seated.

The earlier sections of Cheonma's diary had taught him to perceive Qi. They had described the natural cycle and the importance of allowing the body to recognise what already existed within it.

This scripture seemed to question the very foundation of that approach.

It did not ask him to become closer to Heaven.

It asked what would happen if he refused to follow its order.

Ajax glanced at the circular diagram once more.

Heaven.

Earth.

The returning cycle.

Then he looked at the second illustration, where the natural flow passed through the mind and emerged altered.

"...So that's why."

He leaned back.

The explanation was incomplete.

The method was vague.

And the experiment, if it could even be called that, seemed to have been recorded in fragments.

But one thing was clear.

Cheonma had not written this as a conventional cultivation technique.

He had written it as a question.

A question that he had attempted to answer through experimentation.

Ajax placed the scripture beside the brown diary.

He looked at both books for a long moment.

One had taught him to feel Qi.

The other proposed that he change its natural order.

He glanced towards the endless sky.

"...You certainly chose an interesting subject."

The stars remained silent.

And Ajax, having reached the end of the scripture, began reading it again.
