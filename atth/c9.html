Against the Heavens · Chapter 9

The Threads Within

1,548 words
Reading on

Two weeks passed.

Ajax had stopped counting the number of attempts.

At first, he had recorded every successful experiment, every failure, and every minor variation in the sensations he experienced. He had even begun noting the time of day, the condition of the energy crystals, and the duration of each meditation.

Eventually, however, the records became too numerous.

There was always something else to observe.

A slight difference in the sensation after waking up. A change in its intensity following a meal. An unfamiliar impression while sitting near an enchanted household device. Even the subtle distinction between the energy released by a crystal and the sensation he occasionally encountered within himself.

He wrote them all down.

His private diary had become a rather important companion.

*

The first significant change came during an ordinary morning.

Ajax sat beside his bedroom window, following the familiar routine.

He had a small energy crystal placed on the table. Its stored energy had been released several times already, leaving only a faint residue.

He closed his eyes.

He did not attempt to hold the sensation.

Instead, he allowed it to appear and disappear.

A brief attempt.

A pause.

Then another.

He repeated the process.

At first, there was little difference from his previous exercises. Sometimes he recognised the sensation immediately; sometimes it took several attempts. On particularly unproductive mornings, he could spend nearly an hour without noticing anything meaningful.

But he continued.

He had begun to suspect that his earlier approach was fundamentally mistaken.

He had treated the sensation as something that needed to be caught and maintained. Every time it appeared, he would concentrate on it, trying to prevent it from disappearing.

That had made the entire process unnecessarily difficult.

Perhaps the problem was not the sensation.

Perhaps it was the way he approached it.

Instead of trying to sustain it, he began training himself to recognise it repeatedly.

He would perceive it for a moment, relax his attention, and try again.

He did this dozens of times.

Then hundreds.

The duration of each successful attempt mattered less than the consistency with which he could recognise the sensation.

It was a simple adjustment.

The results, however, were not.

*

A few days later, Ajax noticed something unusual.

He had been following the sensation for several minutes when he became aware of something beyond it.

Until then, he had only been able to recognise a vague presence within his body. It was difficult to distinguish its location, and even more difficult to understand its movement.

Now, something seemed to be taking shape.

He stopped.

Then resumed the exercise.

The sensation appeared again.

This time, he did not attempt to follow it immediately. He allowed his awareness to settle around it, observing its subtle changes.

There.

Something was moving.

Or perhaps it had always been moving, and he had only just begun to notice.

Ajax concentrated on the sensation.

It seemed to extend through his body in countless directions.

Thin.

Faint.

Interconnected.

Like an enormous network of wires, each one running alongside another, branching and meeting at places he could not properly identify.

He could not see them.

It was not a visual phenomenon.

Rather, his mind was beginning to interpret the sensation through a familiar image.

Countless threads.

Countless connections.

Ajax remained still.

He carefully followed one of them.

For a brief moment, he could distinguish its direction before it became indistinct among the others.

His eyes opened.

"...What was that?"

He looked down at himself.

Nothing appeared different.

He moved his fingers, then his arms. Everything seemed as it had before.

Yet the sensation remained in his memory with unusual clarity.

He reached for his private diary.

This time, he did not begin with a written description.

He picked up a pencil and started drawing.

The first sketch was little more than a collection of lines. He marked the approximate locations where the sensations seemed strongest, then drew several branching connections between them.

He paused.

The result looked rather strange.

It resembled an anatomical diagram drawn by someone who had never studied anatomy.

Ajax examined it.

"...Not particularly convincing."

He turned the page and tried again.

This time, he divided the sketch into several sections, using different patterns to distinguish the sensations he had experienced.

He marked the areas he could recognise with confidence and left the rest incomplete.

At the bottom, he wrote:

The perceived sensation resembles an interconnected network extending throughout the body.

The apparent connections are numerous and difficult to distinguish individually.

This is an interpretation of the sensation, not a confirmed physical structure.

He underlined the final sentence.

He had no intention of mistaking an impression for an established fact.

Still, the sketch was worth keeping.

*

The following days brought another change.

Ajax no longer needed to sit in perfect stillness to recognise the sensation.

He continued practising meditation, of course. It remained useful for observing the more subtle aspects of Qi.

But the sensation itself had started appearing during ordinary activities.

At first, it happened accidentally.

While drinking water, he noticed a faint movement within his body.

While eating breakfast, he became aware of the same interconnected sensation.

Even when reading, he occasionally found himself recognising it without deliberately attempting to do so.

The first few times, he stopped whatever he was doing and tried to examine it.

That was a mistake.

The moment he redirected all his attention towards it, the sensation became difficult to distinguish.

So he changed his approach again.

He continued eating.

He continued reading.

He allowed the sensation to remain in the background of his awareness.

Gradually, it became easier.

He could recognise it without interrupting whatever he was doing.

It was no longer necessary to enter a state of intense concentration every time he wished to perceive it.

His body was becoming accustomed to the sensation.

Or, more accurately, he was becoming accustomed to perceiving something that had probably been present all along.

He still could not explain exactly what he was feeling.

He could not command it.

He could not deliberately alter its movement.

And he certainly could not claim to understand the network he had sketched.

But he could feel it.

That alone was a considerable improvement.

*

His private diary continued to fill.

One page contained observations about the differences between external energy and the sensation within his body.

Another contained a list of experiments involving energy crystals.

Several pages were devoted to the descriptions found in Cheonma's diary, alongside Ajax's own interpretations.

There were diagrams, corrections, crossed-out sentences, and questions that had yet to receive answers.

Some entries were only a few lines long.

Others occupied entire pages.

He had begun writing whenever he noticed something unusual, regardless of whether he was in the middle of an experiment.

Consequently, his handwriting had become increasingly inconsistent.

A few entries were written in haste, while others were carefully arranged.

He occasionally returned to older observations and added corrections.

Sometimes, he found that an assumption he had made several days earlier was no longer convincing.

He would cross it out and write a new explanation beneath it.

The process continued.

And continued.

Until one evening, Ajax reached for his diary and discovered something unexpected.

There were no empty pages left.

He turned the book over.

Then opened it again.

He checked the final page, followed by the one before it.

Every page had been used.

He stared at the book for a moment.

"...When did that happen?"

He had not realised how much he had written.

For a while, he simply examined the pages.

The first entries were brief and uncertain. They contained descriptions of sensations he could barely distinguish.

The later ones were considerably more detailed.

There were diagrams of the perceived network, comparisons between different sensations, and notes concerning the circumstances in which he could recognise Qi without deliberate concentration.

He had also filled several pages with questions.

Ajax turned to the first page again.

It was strange.

He had started with almost nothing.

A vague sensation produced by an energy crystal.

And now, two weeks later, he had filled an entire diary trying to understand it.

He closed the book.

Perhaps he needed another one.

*

That night, Ajax returned to the familiar expanse beneath the stars.

The stone table stood in the same place.

Cheonma's brown diary rested upon it.

Ajax approached and opened it, intending to examine the passages he had encountered during the previous nights.

But something was different.

There was another book on the table.

It was considerably thicker than the diary.

Its cover was dark, with no visible decorations. Unlike Cheonma's worn leather diary, its surface appeared almost untouched.

Ajax stopped.

He had not seen it there before.

He looked around the empty expanse.

Nothing moved.

No new system window appeared.

There was no voice announcing another inheritance or explaining what he had received.

Only the book.

Ajax approached the table.

He reached out and placed his hand upon its cover.

The title was written clearly across the front.

Reverse Heaven Divine Scripture.

He read it once.

Then again.

His gaze shifted towards Cheonma's diary.

"...Another one?"

He pulled out the chair and sat down.

For a few moments, he simply looked at the unfamiliar book.

Then he opened it.

And began to read.
