Cheonma

Status: the antagonist-mentor. Deceased before the story begins. Author of Ajax's inheritance.

Confirmed from the diary (ch3, ch4, ch10)

Voice

Personal, untidy, full of corrections — sentences struck out, rewritten, struck out again. "Someone arguing with himself in ink." Later sections grow neater and sharper: "the hand of a man who had stopped arguing and started deciding."

His argument

  • "I have spent too many years looking upward."
  • "The heavens do not answer, nor do they care to."
  • "I have mistaken strength for sovereignty. I have mistaken silence for peace. I have mistaken the heavens for something beyond myself."
  • "Anything given for free, is a shackle. Everything sweet is poison."
  • "A man with no self is a river. Whatever falls into him, he carries. Do not be a river. Be a gate."
  • "To read the heavens, I must first learn to read myself."
  • "They say the heavens decide who may ascend... I disagree."
  • "No hand will come down from above to grant me permission. No door will open because I waited politely outside it. And if it is the heavens themselves that stand in my way, then I shall cut the heavens themselves."
  • "I once wanted to reach the heavens. To become one with that divine realm. But now I know. That can never happen, not until one of us yields. Whatever those orthodox idiots call me, I do not care. I will not go down, no matter what the heavens throw at me."

From the Scripture (ch10)

  • Qi descends, returns. The cultivator who follows the cycle grows purer as the mind approaches heaven.
  • He asked "Why?" — why should purity of mind determine purity of Qi?
  • "The self distinguishes. The self interprets. The self imposes. What passes through it may no longer remain unchanged."
  • "The mind may be a filter."
  • "I attempted to impose the self upon the natural flow. The Qi changed. It did not become pure. It became mine."
  • "Natural Qi follows Heaven. The self imposes its order. The flow is altered. The result is Demonic Qi."
  • "I have not understood the method. I have only confirmed the possibility. The self must become the source of the imposed order. Until then, the change remains superficial."

What is known about him

  • He spent three months in the wilderness observing nature before he could perceive Qi (ch6)
  • Historians are uncertain whether Cheonma is a name or a title (ch6)
  • Different records call him a wandering ascetic, or a man who rejected the authority of the heavens
  • He is called a heretic by "those orthodox idiots"
  • The stars flickered and the diary shuddered when Ajax spoke his name; Ajax felt something like "standing at the foot of an enormous mountain and understanding, for the first time, that he had been mistaking the foothills for the world"

The critical thing Ajax does not know

Cheonma's method stops Qi from returning to heaven. Ajax reads this as philosophy. In the setting's terms it is theft — see The Two Heavens. Cheonma knew exactly what he was doing. Ajax does not.

This asymmetry is the engine of volume 1. Ajax is being taught by a dead man to commit a crime he has not been told is a crime.

His style on the page

Cheonma writes to himself. He compresses philosophy into two or three lines because he does not need to convince himself. He describes methods vaguely because they were never separable from him. He leaves blanks — some deliberate, some because he never got round to them.

He is not hiding anything from Ajax. He is simply not talking to Ajax at all.

Consequence for the prose: never have Cheonma conveniently answer the question Ajax needs. Every gap must be filled by Ajax's own experiment, and the failures must be shown. See The Demonic Path.

What Cheonma got wrong (PROPOSED)

He says he "confirmed the possibility" but "not understood the method." He never states the method.

Recommendation: he did find it eventually — the Scripture is what he left behind — and the reason it is a diary of questions rather than a manual is that the method cannot be transmitted, only re-derived. Each practitioner must build it from their own self. That makes Ajax's struggle necessary rather than an information deficit, and it makes Cheonma's failure at the end meaningful rather than accidental.

Open questions

  • Is he alive? UNRESOLVED — the diary reads like a last testament, but nothing confirms death
  • Was he human? UNRESOLVED
  • Why did the inheritance come to a paralysed boy in a provincial republic? UNRESOLVED — this is one of the six mysteries
  • What does "not until one of us yields" mean? Who is the other party? UNRESOLVED, and probably a late-volume revelation
