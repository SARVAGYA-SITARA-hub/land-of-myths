The Continent

Status: PROPOSED. Nothing here is canon until it appears in a chapter.

Scale correction (author): the Republic is one small country with five governors. Altos is not in the Republic — it sits in the Central Kingdom. The world is large and includes floating islands. This note has been rebuilt to that scale.

Design principle

The map is the record. Geography is not scenery — it is the physical form of the ledger.

Every feature should answer a question about who controls the flow of what is owed. Rivers are how rent moves. Mountains are where the register does not reach. Coasts are where accounts can be questioned. The map therefore is the political system, drawn to scale.

See The Two Heavens and Threads of Fate.

The Republic's smallness is a story engine, not a detail. Ajax is a governor's son — a large man in a small country. The moment he leaves, he is nobody. That is a better humiliation than being a prince in a great kingdom, and it makes the academy genuinely frightening.

The belts

I. The north-west — the Republic

A small river valley on the north-west coast. Five governorships, one crown, an annual reception, and a settled conviction that it matters.

  • Its river — the Cael — descends from the Ilvan foothills and empties into the northern sea.
  • The Republic is not the continent's breadbasket or arbiter. It exports a little, imports a great deal, and is watched by nobody.
  • This is why the first calamity is so devastating there: nowhere important sends help quickly.
  • Divination is licensed here. Reading fate without a licence is treason.
  • This is Ajax's home. He begins at the top of a very small ledger and is written out of it.

II. The centre — the Central Kingdom

The hinge of the world. Where roads meet, where foundries and maritime exchanges are, where the academy is.

  • Not one culture — a trading seam where river law and sea custom both half-apply.
  • Crowded, unregistered, impatient. People come here to be lost or made.
  • Altos Institute is here. So is Veylan, and the shipping that carried the parasite.

III. The south — the Lahir Empire

Old, warm, confident. Magic is a profession here, not a heresy. Mora's Council of Magic is its crown jewel, and the place Zhen is trying to reach.

IV. The east — the Ilvan Range and after

A mountain spine halving the continent. Beyond it, the Unassessed: no clerks, no diviners, no assessment.

  • This is where a dead man walks unrecorded.
  • The Republic's fifth governorship technically sits in the western foothills — the poorest seat, let to whoever will take it.

V. Above — the Hanging Isles

Floating islands. Visible from the ground, unreachable by road, and the place the setting says heavenly Qi enters the world.

This is the most important addition to the setting, because it makes the metaphysics physical:

  • The cycle in Reverse Heaven Divine Scripture — Qi descends, returns — now has an address. It comes down at the Isles.
  • Cheonma's theft is no longer abstract: there are places where you can stand under the leak.
  • The Register is kept about the Isles, even though the Isles themselves cannot be visited.
  • The Isles drift. Slowly — but they drift. Where Qi enters the world changes over centuries, and with it which lands are blessed and which are abandoned. That is an entire history of wars, free of charge.

Each needs somewhere to happen, and the scale lets the disasters be geographically distinct rather than all landing on one city.

Do not fix these yet. But note the shape: the six escalate from biological to metaphysical, and geographically they spiral outward from Ajax's home to the origin of Qi itself. That gives you a series arc a reader can feel without a line of exposition.

Distances

Proposed: the Republic to the Central Kingdom is six weeks by carriage, three by fast post. That makes "two months to term" (ch4) generous, "a month before departure" (ch5, ch9) consistent, and the journey long enough that a carriage can vanish in the middle of it and no one hear for weeks.

What to decide before drawing again

  1. Where does the Register physically live? A place, an institution, or something stranger. This decides the political centre of gravity.
  2. Do the Isles move on the timescale of the story? If yes, one calamity is a landfall and you have a clock.
  3. Is there a second continent? A world with floating islands probably has more than one shore.
