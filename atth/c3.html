Against the Heavens · Chapter 3

Beginner’s Pack

1,524 words
Reading on

The words did not change when he read them a second time.

Ajax checked a third time, purely on principle.

[Dual Nurture Program] hung in the dark exactly where it had been, glowing faintly.

He had always considered dreams rather unpleasant. Most were scraps of the day stitched together by a mind that had forgotten restraint. Sometimes he dreamt of falling. Sometimes he dreamt of walking.Once, memorably, his father had turned into a particularly angry goose.

This was nothing like those.

The darkness around him wasn’t empty. Stars hung in it from every side, some blazing, some so faint he could only see them by not looking directly, and they were arranged in patterns that felt less like scattered light and more like a painting.

He was standing.

He had been standing for some time, he realised.

Ajax looked down.

There was no wheelchair. There were his legs, and his feet, planted on a floor like a perfectly still lake that reflected no stars. He could feel it: the faint cool of the surface under his heels, the weight of his own knees. Not the dull, borrowed awareness he had learned to live with. Actual feeling.

He didn’t move.

Hope was a dangerous habit. He had broken himself of it at considerable cost, and he had no intention of relapsing over a dream.

Then, very slowly, he curled his toes.

They curled.

“Woah….” He exclaimed as he wobbled while trying to walk in a straight line.

One step, and then another.

“Don’t wake up,” he told whichever part of him was in charge of that. “Please. Just this once.”

Nothing answered. But the dream, to its credit, did not end.

He took a third step, and a fourth, and then, because no one was watching, a clumsy, ridiculous little jog that sent soft ripples of starlight spreading across the floor.

“Ha..haha…hehehe..”

He laughed while picking up his pace. Half in amazement and half in disbelief.

When he finally stopped, breathing hard, the window was waiting for him. Not impatiently.

[Conditions met.]

[Initiating compatibility assessment.]

The window vanished, apparently regarding the question as rhetorical.

For a moment nothing happened. Then two clusters of stars began to drift out of the dark.

The first was black.

It did not shine so much as swallow. The stars around it dimmed as it passed, as if it were drinking their light. Its points were scattered, jagged, almost quarrelsome.

The second was bright.

Its stars were joined by fine silver threads, tight and exact, a lattice that reminded him of the inside of a clock. It turned slowly as it approached, precise as a mechanism and about as warm as one.

The two formations drifted toward each other and stopped.

A new window unfolded.

Compatibility Assessment

Inheritance A — Compatibility [89%].

Inheritance B — Compatibility [11%].

Ajax read it twice.

He had been a reasonably good student. Even he could tell which of those was a passing grade.

[Merging Inheritance A.]

[Phase one Loading…………….]

Phase One: Initiated.

The bright cluster withdrew without any visible hard feelings, like a guest who had just realised it was at the wrong table. The black one did the opposite. It came closer. Ajax felt the stars lean over him, and for one very long second he was certain that something vast, and not at all unfriendly, had turned over in its sleep.

Then it was gone, and the dark was only dark again.

“Inheritance?” he said to the empty sky. “Whose inheritance? Who is—”

[Beginner’s Pack: Granted]

Something took shape in front of him, and it was a diary.

Not a divine relic. Not a legendary treasure. A diary: old brown leather gone soft at the corners, a metal clasp darkened with age, no title, no crest, no markings of any kind. It looked like something one might find at the bottom of a drawer, beneath spare buttons.

Almost insultingly ordinary.

Ajax reached for it. His fingers touched the cover. It was rough and a bit hard unlike ordinary leather.

He frowned.

“Quite real for a dream.” he held the diary in his hands.

It had weight and smelled faintly of ink and old rain that perhaps waited for someone to pick it up.

He undid the clasp.

The first page was packed with dense, slanting script in characters he had never seen. They were not the alphabet of his own country, nor any of the foreign languages he had seen. And still the meaning settled in his mind like water finding its level, as though he had known the language once and merely forgotten.

To whoever finds this, If you are reading this, that can only mean one of the two things. Either I have failed in my quest or that the mighty wheel of time doesn’t concern me anymore.

Ajax raised an eyebrow. An unusual way to open a diary.

I have spent too many years looking upward.

The heavens are vast. Ordinary men call them divine, fear them as unknowable, and study them as though their secrets are inexhaustible. The writer left a blank space as if deciding to write ahead or not. I too once believed that if I looked long enough, I would understand or that my efforts would be acknowledged.

But I was wrong.

He turned the page.

The writing was personal and untidy, full of corrections: sentences struck out, rewritten, struck out again. Someone arguing with himself in ink.

The first lesson I learnt was simple: the heavens do not answer, nor do they care to.

The second was harder to accept for me: perhaps my approach was in itself fundamentally flawed.

Ajax went very still.

A few hours ago, though it felt like another life, he had sat on a balcony with his hand raised to the stars and asked what crime he had committed to deserve this. They hadn’t answered him either.

He swallowed, and read on more slowly.

The diary went on, talking about everything. The meaning of life, fate and that if everything was predetermined then why was human effort necessary? Who gave them the authority to toy with a human life?

Ajax felt that either this diary is written by a madman or a philosopher, a great one at that.

But after reading the writer’s argument Ajax could feel: sadness and perhaps denial. It felt like reading a child’s diary who had become very stubborn and now cannot be lured away with chocolates or little rewards anymore.  This writer questioned everything. Why did the body obey the mind? Why did fear interrupt a movement already begun? Why did a man hesitate, even when he knew exactly what he wanted?

And why did the heavens look so orderly from far away, and so chaotic up close?

Between the questions came confessions, gloriously unflattering.

I have mistaken strength for sovereignty.

I have mistaken silence for peace.

I have mistaken the heavens for something beyond myself.

Ajax nearly smiled. Whoever this was, he was at least honest, which put him ahead of most of the people Ajax had met at formal gatherings.

Further on, the handwriting changed. It grew neater. Sharper. The hand of a man who had stopped arguing and started deciding.

Anything given for free, is a shackle. Everything sweet is poison. The heavens do not hate us. That is the trouble. They simply do not care.

A man with no self is a river. Whatever falls into him, he carries. Do not be a river.

Be a gate.

Below it, a line had been crossed out so violently that only one sentence survived.

To read the heavens, I must first learn to read myself.

Ajax looked at it for a long time.

It wasn’t even a remarkable sentence. He had read grander ones. But the letters had been pressed so hard into the page that they left grooves, and something in the plain fury of those grooves made the words feel less like philosophy and more like a door being kicked open.

He turned to the last written page. Only a few lines.

I once wanted to reach the heavens. To become one with that divine realm. But now I know. That can never happen, not until one of us yeilds. Whatever those orthodox idiots call me, I do not care. I will not go down, no matter what heavens throw at me.

And beneath it, a name.

Ajax sounded it out carefully. “Ch… **Cheonma**?”

The stars flickered.

The diary shuddered in his hands, and for one instant Ajax felt something that was not a voice and not a thought. It was closer to standing at the foot of an enormous mountain and understanding, for the first time, that he had been mistaking the foothills for the world.

Then it was gone. [Phase One: Interrupted] [Progress: 1/3]

[Inheritance: Dormant.]

“Interrupted?” Ajax said. “By whom? I didn’t do anything—”

The pages slid out of his fingers like smoke. The floor went soft. The stars smeared into long, drifting streaks, and he grabbed for the diary and caught nothing at all, and then woke up.
