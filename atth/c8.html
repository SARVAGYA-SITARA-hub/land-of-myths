Against the Heavens · Chapter 8

A Faint Trace

1,782 words
Reading on

Ten days had passed since Ajax first began noticing traces of energy without the assistance of a crystal.

Ten days of experiments, observations, and considerably more patience than he had initially expected to require.

His progress, however, remained difficult to measure.

He could recognise the sensation now.

Not consistently, and certainly not whenever he wished, but he had begun to distinguish it from the ordinary impressions produced by his surroundings. It was faint, almost imperceptible, and disappeared whenever he tried too hard to focus on it.

That last part was particularly troublesome.

Every attempt to grasp the sensation seemed to make it slip away.

Ajax sat beneath the orchid trees in the estate garden, a small energy crystal resting in his palm.

The morning was quiet.

A few servants passed along the distant pathway, carrying baskets and speaking in low voices. Beyond the estate walls, the occasional sound of a carriage or automobile drifted through the air.

He paid little attention to them.

His eyes remained closed.

He released a small amount of energy from the crystal.

The sensation came almost immediately.

A faint disturbance brushed against his awareness. It was familiar now, though still difficult to describe. It did not resemble warmth, pressure, or the movement of air against his skin.

It was simply there.

Ajax concentrated on it.

And it vanished.

He opened one eye.

"...Still rather uncooperative."

He turned the crystal between his fingers.

The experiment was becoming repetitive. He would stimulate his senses, recognise the sensation, attempt to follow it, and lose it the moment he tried to hold on.

Sometimes he could recognise a trace left behind after the energy had dissipated. On other occasions, he could barely distinguish anything at all.

Still, the results were not entirely useless.

He had started to notice that the sensation produced by the crystal was not quite the same as the one he occasionally encountered during meditation.

They shared certain characteristics, but there were differences.

The crystal's energy was easier to perceive. It was more distinct, almost as though something had briefly announced its presence before disappearing.

The other sensation was much quieter.

It did not announce itself.

It seemed to exist beneath the threshold of his ordinary awareness, waiting to be noticed.

Ajax placed the crystal beside him.

Then he resumed his meditation.

*

His daily routine had changed considerably.

He no longer spent as much time in the library or the estate's common areas. Whenever someone asked about him, the answer was usually the same.

Young Master Ajax was reading.

Or resting.

Occasionally, both.

His family did not object. Ajax had always enjoyed spending time with books, and his upcoming departure for the Central Academy provided a convenient explanation for his sudden interest in remaining indoors.

Elena, however, was less convinced.

"You have been reading for three days."

Ajax looked up from the book resting on his lap.

"An impressive observation."

"You're not even turning the pages!"

"I am contemplating the contents."

"You're looking at the same page."

"Some passages deserve more consideration than others."

Elena narrowed her eyes.

Ajax returned to his book.

She stared at him for another moment before deciding that arguing with her brother was unlikely to produce anything useful.

"You're strange."

"I have been told."

She left shortly afterwards.

Ajax waited until her footsteps disappeared down the corridor before closing the book.

The book had, in fact, remained on the same page for nearly an hour.

He glanced towards the window.

The garden was visible beyond it, the orchid trees swaying gently in the breeze.

There was no particular reason to continue practising outside. His room was quiet, and the occasional interruption could easily be avoided.

Besides, he had begun to realise that the exercises required considerably more time than he had initially anticipated.

He had no intention of wasting it.

*

The recurring dreams had continued.

Every night, Ajax returned to the endless expanse beneath the stars.

The stone table remained where it had always been. Cheonma's brown diary rested upon it, and its pages continued to reveal themselves gradually.

During the past ten days, Ajax had encountered several sections that he had previously overlooked.

Some concerned the nature of Qi.

Others discussed the relationship between the body, the mind, and the natural world.

He had also found several passages describing sensations that Cheonma had experienced during his own early attempts at cultivation.

Ajax found those particularly interesting.

Cheonma's descriptions were not identical to what he had experienced.

In one passage, he described Qi as a presence that could be distinguished from the ordinary sensations of the body. In another, he compared its perception to recognising something that had always existed around oneself but had previously gone unnoticed.

There were references to subtle changes in the surrounding environment, too.

None of the descriptions provided a precise explanation.

Some were written as observations. Others were questions, followed by incomplete thoughts.

Ajax had initially expected the diary to contain a clear explanation of what he was supposed to feel.

Instead, it offered him several accounts of what Cheonma himself had experienced.

That distinction mattered.

He could not simply assume that his own sensations would be identical.

Perhaps their bodies perceived Qi differently. Perhaps the circumstances were different. Or perhaps he had misunderstood something in the descriptions.

He had no way of knowing yet.

One passage caught his attention.

Do not mistake the first sensation for the thing itself.

The body recognises what the mind has not yet understood.

Observe the difference. Do not force the two to become one.

Ajax read the lines again.

Then he turned the page.

There was no further explanation.

"...You really do enjoy leaving things unfinished."

The diary, naturally, offered no response.

*

The following morning, Ajax began another experiment.

He placed a small energy crystal on the table beside him and settled into his usual position.

His private diary lay open nearby.

He had already recorded the previous attempts, including the circumstances in which the sensation had appeared and the reasons it had disappeared.

Today, he intended to approach the exercise differently.

Rather than trying to reproduce the sensation and immediately follow it, he would attempt to remain aware of it without interfering.

He closed his eyes.

His breathing gradually settled.

The sounds outside his room became less important. He could still hear them, but they no longer demanded his attention.

He released a small amount of energy from the crystal.

There.

A faint impression appeared at the edge of his awareness.

Ajax recognised it.

He did not move towards it.

He did not attempt to grasp it or force his attention upon it.

He simply observed.

For a moment, the sensation remained.

Then it began to fade.

Ajax felt the familiar urge to concentrate harder.

He resisted it.

The sensation grew weaker.

And weaker.

Until—

Nothing.

He opened his eyes.

It had lasted longer than before.

Not by much, perhaps, but enough to make him reconsider his approach.

He looked at the crystal.

Then at his diary.

He wrote down the duration of the attempt and added a short observation.

Attempt: Maintain awareness of the sensation without deliberately pursuing it.

Result: The sensation persisted for longer than in previous experiments.

Possible conclusion: Excessive concentration may interfere with perception.

He paused.

Then added:

Not confirmed.

Ajax put down the pen.

He would need to repeat the experiment.

It took several more attempts.

Some failed almost immediately. Others produced the same brief impression he had experienced over the previous ten days.

But gradually, something changed.

Ajax began to understand that the sensation did not need to be pursued.

Whenever he tried to seize it, his attention became too narrow. He would focus on the sensation itself and lose awareness of everything surrounding it.

When he allowed his attention to settle, however, the faint impression sometimes remained.

It was a delicate balance.

He could not ignore it, but neither could he force himself to perceive it.

He needed to remain aware without becoming impatient.

The next attempt began like the others.

The crystal released its stored energy.

A familiar sensation brushed against his awareness.

Ajax recognised it.

His breathing remained steady.

He allowed the sensation to remain in the background of his awareness, neither chasing it nor deliberately pushing it away.

Seconds passed.

Then more.

The sensation weakened, but it did not disappear.

Ajax's fingers twitched slightly.

He ignored them.

For the first time, he could distinguish the sensation from the memory of having felt it.

It was not merely the recollection of the crystal's energy.

Something was still there.

A faint trace.

He remained still.

Then, very carefully, he began to follow it.

Not with his hands.

Not by moving his body.

He simply directed his attention towards the sensation, attempting to understand where it seemed to originate and how it changed.

It was like following a barely visible thread in the darkness.

He could not see its beginning.

He could not determine where it ended.

But he could follow it.

For several moments, the sensation remained intact.

Then it disappeared.

Ajax opened his eyes.

He did not move immediately.

His gaze rested on the crystal.

Then on his hands.

A quiet smile almost formed on his face, though he suppressed it before it could fully appear.

"...So that's possible."

He reached for his diary.

His hand moved a little faster than usual as he turned to a clean page.

Experiment: Maintaining the sensation during meditation.

Result: Successful, once.

Observation: For the first time, I was able to retain the sensation without immediately losing it through excessive concentration.

He stopped writing.

There was something else.

He had not merely felt the energy.

He had followed it.

Even if only briefly.

He added another line.

The sensation appears to have a traceable direction or pattern. Further observation is required.

He read the entry twice.

Then he underlined the final sentence.

He had no idea whether the sensation was truly Qi, whether it originated within his body, or whether he had simply discovered another aspect of the energy released by the crystal.

There were still too many uncertainties.

But ten days ago, he could barely recognise anything without deliberately stimulating his senses.

Now, for the first time, he had managed to maintain the sensation through meditation and follow it for several moments.

It was not mastery.

It was not even control.

But it was something he could build upon.

Ajax closed the diary and glanced towards the window.

The morning light had shifted across the floor.

He had been sitting there for considerably longer than he intended.

"...I suppose breakfast can wait."

He picked up the crystal again.

There was still much to learn.
