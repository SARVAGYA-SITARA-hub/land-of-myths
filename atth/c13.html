Against the Heavens · Chapter 13

The Morning After

1,983 words
Reading on

The first report arrived at the palace shortly before sunrise.

It was initially treated as a medical emergency involving a single guest.

A young minister's son had developed a high fever during the night. His family physician had been summoned, but the boy's condition had worsened rapidly.

He had begun coughing.

There had been blood.

The physician had immediately requested assistance from the Royal Medical Service.

By the time the second report arrived, the first had not yet been resolved.

A military officer who had attended the reception had collapsed at his residence.

Then a member of the royal administrative staff.

Then two members of a merchant family.

The reports were unrelated in appearance.

Different households.

Different physicians.

Different districts.

But the symptoms were disturbingly similar.

*

Ajax woke with a dry throat.

For a moment, he remained still.

His room was quiet.

The curtains were partly open, allowing the morning light to enter.

He tried to swallow.

It hurt.

He frowned.

Perhaps he had slept with his mouth open.

He reached for the glass of water on his bedside table.

The first sip did little to help.

The second brought an unpleasant taste.

Ajax paused.

It was metallic.

He placed the glass down.

A faint pressure had settled behind his ribs, accompanied by an unusual heaviness in his limbs.

He had experienced fatigue before.

This felt different.

He reached for the bell.

A servant entered shortly afterwards.

"Good morning, Young Master."

"Good morning."

The servant approached.

"Would you like breakfast?"

"Perhaps later. Could you bring me some more water?"

"Of course."

She noticed his expression.

"Are you feeling unwell?"

"A little."

"Should I inform Madam?"

Ajax considered saying no.

Then he remembered his father's conversation with him.

"Please do."

The servant bowed and left.

Ajax leaned back against his pillow.

He closed his eyes.

The faint sensation of Qi was still there.

But something about it felt unusual.

He could not explain what.

It was as though the familiar sensation had become difficult to distinguish from the discomfort spreading through his body.

He tried to focus.

A cough interrupted him.

He covered his mouth with a handkerchief.

When he lowered it, a small red stain had appeared on the white cloth.

Ajax stared at it.

For several seconds, he did not move.

Then he pressed the bell again.

*

His mother arrived first.

She was followed by his father and Elena.

"Ajax?"

His mother hurried to his bedside.

"What's wrong?"

"I don't feel particularly well."

His father looked at the handkerchief.

His expression changed.

"How long has this been happening?"

"Only since I woke up."

His mother touched his forehead.

"You have a fever."

Elena stood near the doorway.

"Is he going to be alright?"

Nobody answered immediately.

His father turned towards the servant.

"Call the physician. Now."

The servant hurried away.

His mother sat beside Ajax.

"Does anything hurt?"

"My throat. And my chest feels rather heavy."

"Anything else?"

"I'm tired."

His mother looked at him carefully.

"Did you feel this way last night?"

"No."

"Are you certain?"

"Quite."

His father moved closer.

"Ajax, did you eat anything unusual yesterday?"

Ajax thought.

"The seafood course, perhaps."

His father looked towards his mother.

"Did anyone else eat it?"

"Most of the guests, I believe."

Elena looked confused.

"Why?"

Her father did not answer.

He was already reaching for the communication device on the table.

*

The physician arrived within minutes.

He was an elderly man who had served the Wilhelmshire family for years.

He examined Ajax, asked several questions, and checked his temperature.

His expression grew increasingly serious.

"Has he been coughing?"

"Only recently," his mother answered.

"Any difficulty breathing?"

"Some heaviness in the chest."

The physician nodded.

He examined the handkerchief.

Then he looked at Ajax again.

"I would like to conduct a few tests."

"Is it serious?" his father asked.

"I cannot say yet."

"Then tell me what you know."

The physician hesitated.

"I have received reports of similar symptoms from the city this morning."

Governor Wilhelmshire went still.

"Similar?"

"Several people have fallen ill during the night. Some are experiencing fever, weakness and respiratory distress. A few have also reported blood when coughing."

His mother looked at Ajax.

"How many?"

"I don't know the exact number."

The physician closed his medical case.

"But there are enough cases that the Royal Medical Service has begun coordinating the response."

The room fell silent.

Ajax looked at the physician.

"Did they all attend the reception?"

The physician paused.

"Some did."

That was enough to change the atmosphere.

*

By midmorning, the palace had become a centre of emergency activity.

The Royal Medical Service had received dozens of reports.

The Crown's administrative office was contacting every family whose members had attended the previous evening's reception.

The palace kitchens had been closed.

The banquet staff were being questioned.

The suppliers were being contacted.

And the royal physicians were attempting to determine whether the illness was connected to the dinner.

The first suspicion naturally fell upon the food.

The special seafood shipment was brought under examination.

The Veylan Maritime Exchange was contacted.

Its representatives insisted that the shipment had been properly handled and accompanied by all necessary documents.

The palace's head chef was questioned.

He was bewildered.

"We inspected everything when it arrived."

"Who conducted the inspection?"

"My staff and the company's representatives."

"Were there any irregularities?"

"None that we noticed."

"Did you alter the preparation?"

"No. We followed the instructions supplied with the ingredients. The royal kitchen supervised the entire process."

The investigator looked through the documents.

"Did you notice anything unusual about the seafood?"

"No."

The chef looked exhausted.

"Sir, I have prepared meals for the royal household for twenty-three years. If I had noticed something wrong, I would never have allowed it to reach the tables."

The investigator said nothing.

There was no immediate evidence of negligence.

No one had knowingly served anything dangerous.

And yet people were becoming ill.

*

The news reached the Wilhelmshire estate shortly before noon.

Governor Wilhelmshire had received a direct communication from the Crown.

His face was grave as he read it.

His wife stood beside him.

"What's happened?"

He handed her the document.

She read it.

Then read it again.

"All the families?"

"Not all. But many of those who attended the reception have reported illnesses."

"How many?"

"The number is still increasing."

She looked towards the upper floor.

"Ajax..."

Her husband placed a hand on her shoulder.

"The physician is with him. The Royal Medical Service is sending assistance."

"What if—"

"We don't know yet."

He spoke calmly, but his expression betrayed him.

Elena stood a little distance away.

She had heard enough to understand that something was seriously wrong.

"Mother?"

Her mother turned.

"Yes?"

"Will Ajax be alright?"

The lady of the house knelt before her daughter.

"We're doing everything we can."

Elena looked down.

She had not argued with Ajax that morning.

She had not complained about his reading.

She had not even asked whether he would attend dinner.

For some reason, she wished she had.

*

Ajax was beginning to find the physician's repeated visits irritating.

Not because he disliked the man.

Rather, the questions were becoming repetitive.

"Does your throat hurt?"

"Yes."

"Does the pressure in your chest feel stronger?"

"A little."

"Have you coughed again?"

"Yes."

The physician made another note.

"Do you feel dizzy?"

"Occasionally."

"Any unusual sensations?"

Ajax hesitated.

He looked towards the window.

"I think so."

The physician raised his head.

"What kind?"

Ajax struggled to explain.

He could still feel the faint circulation he had spent weeks learning to recognise.

But the sensation was no longer quite the same.

There was a subtle disturbance.

Not pain.

Not exactly.

Something seemed to be interfering with the familiar pattern.

He could not determine whether the sensation came from his body or from his own difficulty concentrating.

"I can't describe it properly."

"That's alright. Don't force yourself."

The physician checked his pulse again.

Ajax closed his eyes.

For a moment, the room seemed very distant.

Then he felt it.

Something.

A faint pressure that did not seem to belong to him.

He opened his eyes.

The sensation disappeared.

"Doctor?"

"Yes?"

"Have you ever encountered an illness that affects Qi?"

The physician looked at him.

"Many illnesses can interfere with Qi circulation."

"Not quite what I meant."

"What do you mean?"

Ajax looked down at his hands.

"I don't know yet."

The physician exchanged a glance with the attendant.

"Rest for now. We will investigate that later."

Ajax did not argue.

He was beginning to feel rather tired.

*

Elsewhere, the reports continued.

A military commander had been admitted to the Royal Medical Hospital.

A senior official had collapsed in his residence.

Several young students who had attended the reception were now under observation.

Some had developed fever.

Some could barely stand.

Others had begun coughing blood.

The Royal Medical Service was overwhelmed.

And the number of affected households continued to rise.

The Crown issued an emergency order.

Every guest who had attended the reception was to be located, examined and placed under observation.

The palace kitchens were sealed.

The Veylan Maritime Exchange was ordered to suspend the relevant shipment.

The royal physicians were instructed to investigate the possibility of a common source.

But no one could explain why the illness appeared so suddenly.

Or why it seemed to affect people who had been perfectly healthy the previous evening.

*

In the Wilhelmshire residence, the afternoon passed slowly.

Ajax drifted between sleep and wakefulness.

His mother remained beside him.

His father moved between the room and the communication chamber, receiving reports from the Crown.

Elena had been sent away twice.

Both times, she had returned.

Eventually, Ajax opened his eyes and found her sitting near the foot of his bed.

"Elena?"

She immediately stood.

"You're awake!"

"For the moment."

She approached.

"Do you need anything?"

"Water, perhaps."

She brought the glass.

Ajax took a small sip.

Then he looked at her.

"Shouldn't you be downstairs?"

"No."

"That's a rather convincing argument."

Elena sat again.

For a while, neither spoke.

Then she asked quietly:

"Does it hurt?"

Ajax considered the question.

"Somewhat."

"Are you scared?"

He looked at her.

He could have given her a reassuring answer.

He could have said that everything would be fine.

But he did not know that.

So he simply said:

"I don't understand what's happening."

Elena lowered her eyes.

Ajax reached out and placed a hand over hers.

"That doesn't mean we won't."

She nodded.

His mother watched them from the other side of the room.

For the first time that day, she allowed herself to breathe a little more easily.

*

That evening, the Royal Medical Service sent another message.

The number of affected people had increased.

The symptoms were not following any recognised pattern.

Ordinary treatments had produced inconsistent results.

Some patients were stabilising.

Others were worsening.

The physicians could not identify a common illness.

And there was something else.

Several patients had begun reporting unusual sensations.

They described pressure beneath the skin, unfamiliar movements within their bodies.

The reports were dismissed as symptoms of fever and exhaustion.

For now.

*

Ajax lay awake long after the room had grown dark.

His mother had finally been persuaded to rest.

A physician remained outside the door.

The house was unusually quiet.

He closed his eyes and tried to feel the familiar circulation.

It was still there.

But something had changed.

He could not tell whether it was his body, his senses, or the illness itself.

Then, for the briefest moment, he felt something answer.

Ajax opened his eyes.

The room was empty.

He waited.

Nothing happened.

After a while, he looked towards the ceiling.

"...What exactly is wrong with me?"

No one answered.

Far beyond the Wilhelmshire estate, the Crown was searching for the source of an illness that had appeared overnight.

And somewhere beneath the confusion, something had begun to awaken.
