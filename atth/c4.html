Against the Heavens · Chapter 4

One Month

1,984 words
Reading on

Morning arrived without knocking on the door.

Sunlight slipped through the curtains and settled across Ajax's face. Outside, the estate had already begun its daily routine. Servants crossed the courtyard, someone was arguing with a deliveryman, and the distant sound of a cart rolling over the stone path reached his room.

Ajax opened his eyes.

The dream remained unusually clear in his memory. The endless sky. The two formations of stars. The old diary.

And that name.

"Cheonma..."

He repeated it quietly.

It sounded strange. Almost like a name from an old folklore.

He looked towards his bedside table. There was no diary.

Of course, there wasn't. It had been a dream.

Ajax pushed the thought aside and reached for the small bell beside his bed. A servant would come shortly to help him prepare for the day.

There was no point wasting the morning thinking about something that had happened in his sleep.

He had more practical matters to attend to.

In exactly two months the winter will arrive and with that, a new term for the Altos Institute of History and Literature Sciences will start. He had to plan for his departure and also get a thorough understanding of the political standings of his father’s contacts and for the sake of connections he would build in the future there.

His father had mentioned it during the banquet, but the arrangements had been discussed long before that. The academy was located in a foreign country, far from the governor's territory and the familiar comforts of their estate.

It was a prestigious institution. Students from different countries, families, and backgrounds gathered there to study. Though of course it was respected only when it came to administration and engineering sciences.

The curriculum was broad, covering everything from literature and archaeology, to political studies and economics.

His father had already expressed his preference.

Literature and Archaeology.

Ajax did not object.

He enjoyed reading and writing, especially poetry which was his hobby. History offered him the thrill of occasional mystery hidden between the lines of an ancient text. These were the few interests he could pursue without needing someone to assist him.

Besides, his father seemed rather pleased with the idea.

Ajax had learnt that arguing against a pleased father was never a good idea.

He rang the bell.

A servant entered a few moments later.

"Good morning, young master."

"Good morning. Could you bring me the documents listing the political workings and social standing of the Central Plains?"

"Of course."

The servant bowed and left.

Ajax glanced towards the window.

Two months.

It was not a particularly long time, especially when there were preparations to make. He would need to learn about the institute's facilities, understand its social hierarchy, and decide what bricks to lay for his future.

The Wilhelmshire family library was quiet.

It was one of Ajax's favourite places in the estate, though not necessarily because of the books. The library had wide windows overlooking the eastern garden, comfortable chairs, and very few people who bothered him with unnecessary conversation.

Most importantly, nobody expected him to participate in anything.

Ajax sat beside a large table, a book open before him. Several sheets of paper lay scattered around it, along with a notebook and a cup of tea that had long since gone cold.

He was studying the Altos Institute's preliminary curriculum.

There was no entrance examination he needed to worry about. His father's position and the family's arrangements had already secured his admission. Still, he had a month before departure, and doing nothing for an entire month seemed rather wasteful.

Besides, he was curious.

The academy's Archaeology department had an extensive introductory programme. Students were expected to understand historical dating, ancient scripts, the classification of relics, and the basic principles of magical archaeology.

The last subject particularly interested him.

Magical relics were not uncommon in this world. Even ordinary households possessed a few objects that relied on stored magical energy. But the older artifacts were a different matter.

Some were discovered in ruins.

Others were recovered from places where the natural laws behaved strangely.

And a few were so old that nobody could confidently explain how they had been made.

Ajax turned a page.

Introduction to Historical Artifacts and Their Classification.

He read the first few paragraphs, then made a small note in his notebook.

His handwriting was neat, though he occasionally paused to consider a particular word.

A servant entered quietly to replace his tea.

"Would young master like something to eat?"

"Later, thank you."

The servant glanced at the books.

"Studying for the academy?"

"Something like that."

"Surely the academy won't make you work so hard before you've even arrived."

Ajax looked up.

"Then I'll have an advantage."

The servant smiled.

"Of course, young master."

She left.

Ajax returned to his book.

He had no particular ambition to become the academy's most diligent student. He simply liked knowing things before he needed them.

It saved time.

And time, unlike most things, was difficult to recover once wasted.

By the time he finished the introductory chapter, the sunlight had begun to fade.

He closed the book and stretched his fingers.

There were still several sections left, but he could continue tomorrow.

His wheelchair rolled quietly across the floor as he made his way out of the library.

The rest of the day passed without anything particularly interesting happening.

He had dinner with his family, listened to Elena complain about the clothes being prepared for their journey, and spent some time reviewing the academy's prospectus.

Eventually, he returned to his room.

It was an ordinary evening.

At least, it began as one.

The stars appeared again.

Ajax stood in the same endless expanse.

He looked down at his feet.

Then around him.

The vast sky was unchanged. Countless stars stretched into the distance, and the two strange formations remained suspended far above him.

For several seconds, he simply stared.

"...Again?"

He had been expecting another ordinary dream.

Not this one.

He remembered the diary.

More importantly, he remembered waking up the previous morning and finding nothing in his hands.

Ajax turned around.

The old brown diary was resting on a stone table behind him.

He approached it slowly.

It was exactly as he remembered. The worn leather cover, the darkened clasp, and the slightly uneven edges of its pages.

He opened it.

The first entries were still there.

He turned through them, searching for the passage he had read the previous time.

To read the heavens, I must first learn to read myself.

He stopped at the line.

Then he turned another page.

The previous time, the writing had ended shortly after the name.

Now there was more.

Ajax leaned closer.

The handwriting was familiar, though the words seemed to carry a different weight.

They say the heavens decide who may ascend and who possesses the right to transcend.

They say that the stars have already determined the place of every man.

That some are born beneath fortunate constellations, while others are condemned to crawl beneath them.

I disagree.

Ajax read the lines again.

The next passage was written with noticeably heavier strokes.

No one will descend from the heavens to grant me permission.

No divine hand will reach down and open the path before me.

And if it is the heavens themselves that stand in my way, then I shall cut the heavens.

Ajax's eyes narrowed.

He had never read anything quite like it.

The statement was not written as a boast. There was no audience to impress, no opponent to intimidate.

It read like a conclusion.

A man had looked at the world, considered its rules, and decided that he would not accept them merely because they existed.

Ajax turned the page.

For the first time, the diary opened into a clearly marked section.

At the top of the page was a title.

The First Step — To Feel the Qi.

Below it, the writing became more organised.

Before the body may be refined, before the will may be tempered, one must first perceive what already surrounds him.

The world is not empty.

Every breath enters a world that is already moving. Every step is taken upon something that does not cease to exist when one's eyes are closed.

Yet most men remain unaware of it.

Ajax read on.

The writer described Qi as something that existed throughout the natural world. It moved through living things, through the air, and through the land. It was not something that could be summoned simply by wishing for it.

Nor could it be seen by ordinary sight.

The first task was to become aware of it.

The diary contained several exercises.

The first was called Stillness of the Body.

Sit without forcing the breath. Do not attempt to control it. Observe its arrival and departure.

Notice the movement of the chest, the warmth of the air, and the subtle changes that occur when the body rests.

Do not mistake the absence of movement for the absence of activity.

The second was Stillness of the Senses.

Close the eyes.

Listen without searching for a particular sound. Distinguish the near from the distant, the continuous from the intermittent.

When the mind begins to wander, return to what is already present.

The third exercise was shorter.

Observe the world.

The movement of leaves. The warmth of sunlight. The direction of the wind. The rise and fall of water.

Do not merely look at them. Pay attention to the changes that connect one moment to the next.

Ajax paused.

He turned the page.

There were notes on the importance of patience, followed by warnings against forcing sensations that were not there.

Do not imagine Qi and then congratulate yourself for finding it.

The mind is capable of deceiving itself with remarkable ease.

Ajax almost smiled.

That sounded like something his father would say.

He continued reading.

You may feel nothing for days. You may feel nothing for months.

Do not become impatient.

The first step is not to command Qi.

It is to recognise that it exists.

Ajax closed the diary halfway and looked up.

The stars above him were beautiful.

He had seen them during both dreams, but this time he found himself observing them differently.

Their arrangement.

Their distance.

The faint differences in brightness.

He wondered whether the writer had once stood beneath a similar sky.

How long had it taken him to develop this path?

How many years had he spent recording these observations?

And why had the diary been given to him?

There were no answers.

Only another page.

Ajax turned it.

At the bottom, a final sentence had been written.

Do not rush to become someone who commands the heavens. First, learn to notice the world that exists beneath them.

The words settled in his mind.

Then the diary began to fade.

Ajax immediately looked down.

"Wait."

The pages disappeared one after another.

A translucent window appeared.

Initial Synchronisation: In Progress.

Inheritance: Cheonma's Diary.

First Section: Available.

Before Ajax could read anything further, the stars stretched into lines of light.

The world vanished.

*

Morning.

Ajax opened his eyes.

He remained in bed for a few moments, staring at the ceiling.

Then he slowly turned his head towards the bedside table.

Nothing.

He looked at his hands.

Nothing there either.

Yet the words remained in his memory.

The first step is to feel the Qi.

Ajax sat quietly.

He had no idea whether the diary was real or whether his mind had begun repeating an unusually elaborate dream.

But the instructions were simple enough.

He could try them.

There was no harm in sitting quietly and observing his surroundings.

After all, he had plenty of time.

And if it turned out to be nothing more than a dream, he would have lost only a few minutes.

Ajax reached for the bell.

The day had begun.
