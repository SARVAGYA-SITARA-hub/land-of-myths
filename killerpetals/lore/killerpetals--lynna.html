Lynna

Status: The girl he loved. Beyond the reach of the text.

Confirmed

  • Lynna Asherforth — the herald announces her with the surname, which matches

Santino's own house. See the warning below.

  • Silver hair. *"Her hair was silver. That is the thing I have kept. Not her face;

I have lost the face."*

  • Eyes the colour of ice on the palace reservoir in the third month.
  • She had somewhere to be before noon the day of the corridor scene. *"I apologise,

Your Highness, but I have to be somewhere before noon."*

  • She let him hold her hand and then took it back *"not hard — she never did

anything hard, and that was the whole difficulty with her."*

  • At the banquet she wears blue. Santino: *"It was just blue, I thought. Everyone

said blue."*

  • She does not turn when he says her name in the hall.
  • Casimir Vane proposes. Her ears go red. She makes *"a small production of being

uncertain, and did not step backwards."* She accepts.

The one thing he keeps returning to

"Her glare at him was not her glare at me. That is the thing that is still somewhere behind my ribs, even now, even after everything: she can look at him like that and be happy about it, and she never once looked at me like that."

And, in the ballroom:

"She was happy. She was happy with him. She had never been unhappy in any way I could fix."

That second line is the real one. It is not that she chose another man. It is that there was never a wound for him to heal, so there was never a way in.

⚠ Warning — the surname

Lynna's surname is Asherforth, the same as Santino's royal house.

The text never states their relationship. Two readings:

  • Cousins — the romance works, the herald's "two names together" means a match

within the noble network, and the tragedy is one of proximity.

  • Siblings — the entire chapter 2 reads completely differently and the book becomes

something else.

UNRESOLVED. This is the highest-priority question in the vault because it changes what chapter 2 is. Do not write another scene with her until the author answers. See 02 Open Decisions #7.

As a character

Thin on the page by design. Santino has "lost the face." We get hair, eyes, a tone of voice, and a way of being kind about the wrong thing. He loved her and cannot now describe her, which is more honest than a full portrait would be.

Do not flesh her out in the bible before the manuscript does. Anything added here would be invention.
