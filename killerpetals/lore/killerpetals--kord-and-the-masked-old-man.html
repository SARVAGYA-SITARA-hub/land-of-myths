Kord and the masked old man

Status: The execution. Two men, one act.

Kord — the executioner

Confirmed:

  • Big. "Kord, who was a big man, came out with a blade longer than his arm."
  • He wipes the blade down with a cloth, unhurried — *"the way a man wipes a tool he

has used before and will use again."*

  • He takes his place at Santino's side and waits for the hierophant's hand.
  • His sword is the one Santino takes when he wakes. It is still lying on the

platform, "where someone had put it down and not come back for it."

Small but load-bearing: Kord's patience is what makes the scene frightening. He is not enjoying it. He is working.

⚠ "Hierophant" is not the author's word. The text only ever says "the old man in the mask". The term is a working label for this vault. Do not use it in narration.

The masked old man

Confirmed:

  • An old man in a mask, who barks the ritual words: "Ola, hula huh u."
  • He sings the hymn in an old language — *"the old language had been shaped for

something else before it was made to do this."*

  • He raises his hand to the crowd; the drums stop completely; his hand comes down; the

blade follows.

  • He is masked, so the text never gives him a face.

Decision recorded: he stays nameless as a role, not a person. He is not "the priest" and he is not given a name. The mask is the character. Do not name him until the manuscript does.

What the ritual establishes

CONFIRMED: this is an old, practised liturgy. The dome is built for long dwelling, the stone has "the look of a thing that had received", garlands and offerings have been brought for a long time, and the drums come "from maybe twenty places at once." This is not a one-off murder. It is an institution.

IMPLIED: the tribe believes they are feeding something. Whether the rose is what they think it is — or whether something else answers — is the chapter 1 question.
