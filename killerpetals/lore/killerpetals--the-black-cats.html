The Black Cats

Status: A named hazard on the sea routes. One line of text.

The only mention

Chapter 4, Nettle to Ostrand:

"when the black cats cut the route you're meant to be taking, you turn back."

And Ostrand: "I should've listened."

What can be read

  • They "cut" a route — the phrase is about a path being severed, not a ship being attacked.
  • There is a known rule: if the black cats cut your route, turn back.
  • Ostrand ignored the rule and kept going, which is how he found Santino.
  • The crew treats it as ordinary seamanship, not superstition or terror. Nettle says it

"as a matter of fact."

Candidate readings — all UNRESOLVED, none canon

ReadingNote
Weather — a storm pattern sailors nameFits "cut the route" and the total absence of fear in Nettle's voice
Pirates or a raiding fleetFits "cut" and would tie the sea to the political plot
A creature or phenomenonFits the book's supernatural register, but nothing on the page supports it
A banned current or reefFits "you turn back" as pure navigation

Do not decide this. It is one line and the author has not indicated which.

Why it's recorded

Because it is the only reason Santino is alive. If Ostrand had turned back as he should have, the boat would not have been there. Whatever the black cats are, they are the mechanism by which the plot reached him — and that is worth remembering when the author decides whether the meeting was chance or arranged.
