The Royal House of Asherforth

Status: The family that was replaced, or the family that wanted him dead.

Sabine — the queen

Confirmed:

  • Santino's mother. Her voice arrives during the ritual, exactly as she would say it:

"Gosh — how many times do I have to tell you to stop sneaking out of the castle—" and "I heard you skipped the basic swordsmanship class again."

  • "Cross, and fond, and about to laugh." He had hated that tone.
  • She gave him the silver coin at the first banquet she allowed him to attend —

"and which she had not said anything about when she gave it, and which I had carried every day since, because it was hers and because I was sentimental and because it was, in the end, the only thing she ever actually handed me."

IMPLIED: that last clause is doing quiet damage. A mother who hands her son exactly one thing in thirteen years.

Aldric — the king

Confirmed:

  • Santino's father. Brant reports to him.
  • *"Looking at me the way he used to look at me — which was not angry, which was the thing

I had never been able to explain to anyone, because he never once raised his voice and somehow it was always worse."*

  • In the chapter 3 memory: laughing alone at a mirror — *"All the countries of the

eastern continent are now in our grasp, who could even imagine — their country's ruler swapped with our people" — "laughing too much. Then stopping. Then turning his head."*

That memory is the pivot of the whole book. See below.

⚠ The "fakes" — the central unresolved fact

Chapter 1, during the ritual, Santino thinks:

"And then the other hands — the ones that have always been in that room. That Sun symbol. I clenched my teeth so hard something at the back of my jaw gave. The corpses. And those fakes standing behind them, calling themselves my parents."

Chapter 3 gives the father's own recorded voice:

"their country's ruler swapped with our people"

Two readings, and they are different books:

ReadingMeans
ReplacementAldric and Sabine were killed and impersonated. The thing that ordered Santino's execution is the empire, wearing his father's face. He has been hunting the wrong people.
MetaphorThey were always dead to him. "Fakes" is contempt, not fact. His father really did sell him.

UNRESOLVED. The author's own chapter 2 note says the anti-deity is "not evil, only negative" — which leans toward replacement, since a negative force would not need a motive to wear a face.

Do not write chapter 5 assuming either. Ask.
