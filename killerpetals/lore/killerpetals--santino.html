Santino

Status: Protagonist. First-person narrator, thirteen years old, technically dead.

⚠ The name "Santino" does not appear in the manuscript. He is never named aloud. Every character calls him "Your Highness", "boy", "the brat", or "Tanno" (the alias he invents in ch3). The name is the author's working name for him and is used throughout this vault because a note needs a filename. Do not put it in his mouth or in narration until the author confirms it is canon.

Confirmed

  • Prince of Asherforth. Brant calls him "the last of the royal line of Asherforth."
  • Thirteen. Stated in chapter 3: "I was thirteen years old and covered in salt."
  • Pale, lean enough to slip through a crowd. He runs from armed men and nearly

outlasts them.

  • Carries a bronze coin in the prologue and a silver coin at the banquet.

The silver one came from Sabine at the first banquet she let him attend. UNRESOLVED whether these are one coin described twice or two.

  • Cannot swim. Said plainly in chapter 3.
  • His hands are ruined by rowing in chapter 3, and stay ruined.
  • Dressed in branches after the resurrection — bound with something that was

"still moving." He wakes still wearing them.

  • Owns Kord's sword, taken from the altar floor.

What he does that tells us who he is

  • He lies to survive, and it is not a flaw. The drowning in the prologue, the

name Tanno, the birds he was hunting. His first instinct is a performance.

  • He reads a room. On the cliff he works out four options and picks one; he

deliberately looks at the vine so Brant looks too.

  • He uses people's assumptions as weapons. "I'm interested in men" stops a

knife because the man cannot hold two impossible things at once.

  • He is vain. *"I smiled at myself in the mirror... I was, I want to be fair to

the boy I was, worth looking at."* He was arranging to propose for three days.

  • He was careless with people below him. Abel served his family forty-one years

and Santino did not know his name. "If I had remembered his name. Would it have been any different? I do not think so. But I think about it."

  • He is not brave in the ballroom. *"I could have stopped it... My legs did not

move because I was standing in a ballroom watching the girl I loved be happy."*

  • He is ashamed of the order of his own feelings. Relief arrived before disbelief

after the resurrection, and he has never got over that.

Reading of the character

PROPOSED: Santino's defining trait is that he is competent and a coward at the same time — he can get out of a cliff and cannot walk across a ballroom. The book's revenge is therefore not about becoming strong. It is about what he will agree to because he was never willing to say one honest word when it counted.

The thesis line, spoken while kneeling on the altar:

"I will kill them. Every one of them. Even if — in the process — I lose myself."

And the line that lands after his head is off:

"If only that day I had power."

The debt

CONFIRMED: he wakes with "the warm, patient wrongness of being owed" and understands that "a debt had been opened in this room that night, and that I was standing in it, and that it was mine."

UNRESOLVED: what the debt is, who holds it, and what he agreed to while dead. He does not remember agreeing. He does remember asking.
