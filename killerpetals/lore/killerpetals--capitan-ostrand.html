Capitan Ostrand

Status: Fishing captain. The first person to look at Santino and want him.

Confirmed

  • Enormous. His shirt "was losing a war it had been losing for years."
  • Captain of a fishing boat with a crew large enough to howl in unison.
  • Loud by default: "WE ARE GONNA FEAST TODAY, BOYS!", "LET THE CURTAINS GO WILD."
  • Patient with lies. *"He had the patience of a man who has heard every lie and has

come to enjoy them as a kind of performance."* He lets Santino's "hunting birds" story run without correcting it.

  • He orders Santino thrown back — "Keep the fishes. Toss the brat back" — and reverses

when Santino says he cannot swim, after a look Santino cannot measure: "Well. We'd best find a port, then."

  • He dissociates. Chapter 4: standing on his own deck, cup in hand, tea spilling

over his fingers, "and there was nobody in him."

  • Nettle talks him back. *"It was not your mistake. Nothing ever was. Say it back to

me. Say it."* Both their shoulders were shaking.

  • Coming back, he looks at Santino *"the way a man looks at something that has arrived

when he was sure nothing would ever arrive again."*

The dissociation — the important part

CONFIRMED: it reads as an established event, not a new one. The crew "stopped, in the way a deck stops when every man on it has seen this before and knows exactly how long to keep their eyes elsewhere." Nettle has a script for it. This has happened many times.

IMPLIED: the trigger is Santino's presence, or the impossible thing Santino just did (being towed by a dolphin). Ostrand's line "I should've listened" is about the black cats and the route — not about Santino.

UNRESOLVED: whether this is the first visible cost of Santino's oath — someone near him coming apart — or an old grief of Ostrand's that Santino merely witnesses. This distinction decides whether the book is a tragedy about collateral damage. See 02 Open Decisions #10.

What he calls Santino

Nothing yet. He asks the name, gets "Tanno," and does not use it in the text. He says "boy" and "the brat."
