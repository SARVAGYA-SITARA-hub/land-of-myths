Brant

Status: Mercenary swordsman. The one who reported the death.

Confirmed

  • Swordsman, in black throat to boot, with a squad that Santino killed more than

half of before the prologue.

  • He says the same line twice — "There is no use running, Your Highness." — and

Santino notes the second time is "always better for a man like that."

  • He grins, and the grin falters when Santino taunts him about the squad.

"Men who are ashamed make mistakes faster than men who are confident."

  • He is thorough. After the vine snaps and the water closes, *"Brant stood there a

while. He was a thorough man. When there was nothing left to look at he turned around."*

  • He reports to Aldric that the last of the royal line of Asherforth drowned

"with a bronze coin in his pocket and a stranger's arm around his neck."

Why he matters

Brant is the proof of Santino's competence. The prologue's trick works because Brant is good: he sees the vine, he understands a half-beat late, and he yells "Stop him!" already knowing it is too late. A lesser antagonist would make the escape cheap.

He is also the first lie in the book. His report is accurate in every detail and wrong in its conclusion, and it is the reason Santino survives. Brant is not a fool. He was simply lied to by the sea.

Where he is now

UNRESOLVED. He reported to Aldric and is presumably alive. If Santino's revenge list exists, Brant is on it — but Brant was doing a job, not a betrayal, and the text never says Santino blames him. Note the difference: Santino's oath names "those fakes standing behind them", not the men with swords.
