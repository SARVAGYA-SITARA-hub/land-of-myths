The Hunt

Status: The pursuit that opens the book. Ordered by someone. Nobody says who.

Confirmed

  • Brant leads it. A squad of hired men, "black from throat to boot."
  • Santino had already killed more than half of them before the prologue starts. *"You say

that like I haven't just cost you more than half your squad."*

  • They are professionals. They track him through forest, they use arrows, and they do not

stop because he is a prince — the honorific is mockery, not deference.

  • Brant is thorough and reports the death to Aldric personally.
  • They believe he drowned. The bronze coin, the stolen arm, the water — Brant watched it

all happen and reported it as fact.

Who ordered it

UNRESOLVED. Three candidates on the page, none confirmed:

CandidateBasis
Aldric — Santino's fatherBrant reports to him directly.
"The fakes" wearing his parentsSantino's own accusation during the ritual: "those fakes standing behind them."
The empireIf the rulers were swapped, then the order came from whoever swapped them.

Note the distinction that matters: the men with swords are not on Santino's revenge list. His oath names "them" and "those fakes" — the ones behind the corpses — not the mercenaries doing a job. Whether that distinction survives his resurrection is a character question, not a plot one.

The squad's fate

  • More than half dead before the prologue.
  • The dagger man went over the cliff with Santino and drowned.
  • Brant survived. Presumably the rest of the squad too.

UNRESOLVED: is Brant still hunting him, or does he believe the report?

Why the prologue works

Because it is a competent escape that is actually a death. Everything in it is true — the coin, the whispered distraction, the vine, the water — and the conclusion is still wrong. That is the trick the book opens with, and it teaches the reader how to read everything after it: watch what he does, not what he says happened.
