Abel

Status: Servant of House Asherforth for 41 years. The conscience of chapter 2.

Confirmed

  • Forty-one years in the family's service.
  • He dresses Santino for the banquet and says: *"You look absolutely stunning, Your

Highness,"* as a matter of fact — not flattery, a report.

  • He introduces himself with the full old form: *"This humble servant is known by the

name of Abel."*

  • Santino barely hears him. *"It barely half reached me. I was fixing my collar. I

was wondering whether she would be in blue."*

  • He bows "properly, the full bow, the one the old ones still did" and shuts the door

quietly — "which is a harder thing to do than it looks and which he had been doing for my family for forty-one years."

Why he matters

Abel is the only person in the draft who asks for nothing and is given nothing. He is not a plot device; he is the measuring stick. The narrator puts his own indifference on the record:

"If I had treated him any better that day. If I had remembered his name. Would it have been any different? I do not think so. But I think about it. That is the whole of it — I do not think it would have changed anything, and I think about it."

PROPOSED: that paragraph is the moral centre of the chapter. Santino is not ashamed because it changed the outcome. He is ashamed because it didn't.

Open

  • Did Abel survive the purge? He is in the palace when the execution is ordered.

UNRESOLVED.

  • His age, family, and whether he served Santino's mother or father first: not stated.

Note on naming

The name Abel carries a biblical resonance — the acceptable offering, the brother killed by a brother. The text does not invoke it. PROPOSED: if that echo is deliberate it is worth one acknowledgement somewhere; if not, leave it alone.
