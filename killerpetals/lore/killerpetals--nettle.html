Nettle

Status: First mate. The one who keeps the captain upright.

Confirmed

  • Tan cloth wound round his head against the sun.
  • Argues with Ostrand about the sails and loses in the most theatrical way available:

"Capitan, the sheets—" / "LET THE CURTAINS GO WILD."

  • Practical. It was his idea to put Santino in the small boat. *"If we find a port,

we can toss the kid there. Put him down on the quay and be off about our business."*

  • He knows Ostrand's collapse before it starts. He watches him go and says

"Capitan?" twice, then shouts it "like a man calling somebody back from a long way off."

  • He has the words for it. *"It was not your mistake. Nothing ever was. Say it back

to me. Say it." Hands up, not touching — "the way you hold your hands up to a horse."*

  • He calls Santino "the brat" — same as Ostrand.

Why he matters

Nettle is the only character in the draft who acts competently under pressure without being asked. He manages the ship, manages the captain, and manages the strange boy, and he complains the whole time.

In chapter 4, after Ostrand shuts himself in his cabin, Nettle's eyes land on Santino:

"And in that moment I stopped being a joke in a small boat and became something else to somebody — though what, exactly, I would not learn for a very long time, and should not have wanted to."

IMPLIED: Nettle has decided something about Santino. The narrator, telling this from later, knows what and is refusing to say. That is a promise to the reader and it has to be paid.

Open

  • Whether he suspects what Santino is: UNRESOLVED, but he is the one who looked.
