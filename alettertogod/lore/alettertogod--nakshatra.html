Nakshatra

Status: PROPOSED — assembled from two chapters only.

On the page

AgeSchool-going. Exact age not stated.
BuildPale, skinny, lean enough to slip through a crowd "without much effort".
HairShort, messy, black — "the only remarkable thing in him".
EyesBig, black. Dark circles under them, which the narration chooses to point at.
DressBlack blazer with gold patterns on wrist, collar and shoulders; plain white shirt; navy blue necktie; black trousers; brown belt and shoes.
CarriesA brown leather handbag/backpack, at least three compartments.
AddressTwo-storey house with dark blue bedroom walls, marble floors, small front-yard garden, red brick boundary wall.

What he does that tells us who he is

  • He gets up after saying he won't. "I don't want to go today" — then he sits up anyway. That single beat is the most characterising thing in the draft.
  • His hands work without him. Making tea is muscle memory; he does it while still half asleep.
  • He pauses at the empty room opposite his own and waits a minute, "maybe hoping for it to open." He does this every morning, apparently.
  • He notices a date and does arithmetic. 14 August 2034; two years.
  • He stops at the letterbox. Raises his hand. Stops halfway. Leaves.
  • He does not answer roll call. He is at the window, watching nothing in particular.
  • He does not plead when sent out. He goes "obediently without making any plea for forgiveness."
  • He cannot be seen. In the corridor, people pass and "no one seemed to notice him."
  • He pinches his fingers while waiting, "as time started to weigh on his conscience."

Reading of the character

PROPOSED: this is a boy in grief who has built a routine precisely so he does not have to feel it. The tea, the uniform, the walk, the train — all of it is scaffolding. The two things he cannot do are open that door and post that letter.

If that reading is right, the novel is about the day the routine breaks.
